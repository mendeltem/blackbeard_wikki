# Diagram lines never cross boxes or text: route them through free lanes, then look at the rendered picture

## Aussage
1. A connector line (arrow, edge) never runs through a box, a label or another text. It goes around the box through a
   free lane: the gap between two columns, the margin of the picture, or above or below a row.
2. Prefer orthogonal routing (horizontal and vertical segments, `H`/`V` in SVG paths) to curves. With curves it is hard
   to see where a line passes.
3. Two lines that share a lane get their own offsets (for example x = 1260 and x = 1270), so they do not lie on top of each
   other. Where they enter a box they enter at different points.
4. Labels on a line sit next to the line, never on another box or line.
5. Before a diagram is sent or published, render it (for example headless Firefox, `firefox --headless --screenshot`) and
   look at the PNG. The SVG source alone does not show crossings.
6. Diagrams with exact words (system diagrams, workflows) are drawn as vector graphics, not generated by an image model.
   On 2026-10-29 Z-Image Turbo wrote 1 of 3 quoted labels wrong ("CLE SERVER" became "CLEERVER").

## Trägt
- In Purchase's experiment, edge crossings had the strongest effect on how well people understood a graph of all the
  aesthetics tested.
- On 2026-10-29 the first version of the Clara overview diagram had a Telegram line running through the agent and relay
  boxes and a green arrow crossing the System One box. The second version still had two vertical lines through the Clé
  box. The user noticed both from the picture. Both were fixed by moving the lines to free lanes (between the columns,
  then along the right margin).

## Trägt nicht
Dense network plots (hundreds of edges) cannot avoid every crossing; there the rule is to minimise crossings and to
keep text clear of lines.

## Bruchindikator
A reader has to trace a line by hand to see which box it belongs to, or a line covers a word.
