Automatische Erkennung zerebraler Mikroblutungen (CMB) auf T2*-GRE und SWI. Alle Zahlen stammen aus dem oeffentlichen Datensatz VALDO 2021 Task 2 (72 Faelle, drei Kohorten mit 0,8 bis 4 mm Schichtdicke; Sudre et al. 2024, doi:10.1016/j.media.2023.103029), gemessen als Laesions-F1 in 5-fach-Kreuzvalidierung auf 57 Faellen, gepaart ueber Faelle mit 95-%-Bootstrap-Intervall. Ausgangspunkt war MicrobleedNet (Sundaresan et al. 2023, doi:10.3389/fninf.2023.1204186). Keine Zahlen eigener Kohorten; die oeffentliche Seite mit allen Schritten und Quellen: https://mendeltem.github.io/valdo-cmb-qc/architecture.html

Stand 2026-10-12. Ein Eintrag je Datei.

Kurzstand

New 2026-11-01 (slice thickness, external models, clinical threshold): On public VALDO, CMB of the same size (2-5 mm) are found in about 64 % at 0.8-3 mm but 43 % at 4 mm, with no rise in wrong detections; thick slices also remove CMB under 2 mm from image and reference (observation, confounded with site). An external model scored without the own component-size filter and with a threshold search reaching 0 gave sensitivity 0.99 / F1 0.87 -- an artefact of the touch rule. At the four-CMB treatment threshold, newer classifiers (up to +0.08 SWI lesion F1) did not move patient-level specificity; a radiologist grey zone of counts 3-8 raised it by about +0.2 to +0.3 without missing anyone above the threshold, robust to neighbouring and nested bands.

New 2026-10-31 (adapting a public detector to a new site): Unchanged, the public VALDO U-Net keeps sensitivity on a new 5 mm T2* site but produces ~20x the false positives; fine-tuning with 3 local scans recovers most of the gap and is ~+0.05 F1 ahead of training from scratch, the lead shrinks to ~+0.01 from 15 scans, both plateau at ~15 scans (interim, one fold; fine-tune-from-public-valdo-weights). Norm-only fine-tuning is worse. Rule: test threshold re-calibration on the same n scans before crediting fine-tuning (calibrate-before-you-fine-tune). SWI pitfall: a brain mask cut at image > 0 hid ~8 % of reference CMB in phase-mask holes (swi-brain-mask-holes-hide-microbleeds).

New 2026-10-25 (what is a good F1, given rater variance): No absolute target exists; the human band is the yardstick. Six raters against an adjudicated reference reach lesion F1 0.52-0.76 (median 0.69), the same rater twice 0.82; experience moves it most. Derived human band ~0.65-0.80 with precision 0.75-0.90; our chain (~0.65) is in the lower middle, and F1 > 0.85 against one rater points to a lenient reference. Proof of 'human-level' needs a second rater and a paired comparison fixed in advance (human-rater-variance-and-good-f1-for-cmb-2026-10).

New 2026-10-25 (Arena 2, round 4): Three levers the wiki had no entry for, all stage-2 arms. Tabular stage 2 (LightGBM on handcrafted shape, Hessian, contrast and tube features) is the cheap baseline to run first: the only same-candidate comparison found went to handcrafted+RF at ~400 positives, the combination best. Frozen DINOv2/MedSigLIP features only with patch tokens pooled at the candidate, never the CLS token. Unlabelled scans: no CMB paper, analogues give small gains at our label count, more likely lower variance (arena-2-research-round-4-unlabelled-tabular-frozen-2026-10).

New 2026-10-25 (transformers as token sequences): Feeding the volume voxel by voxel (or with 2^3-4^3 patches) to a transformer is possible with position embeddings, but unproven for CMB: one preprint (MedMNIST 28^3) only, no lesion result, Swin UNETR already lost on VALDO. With ~300 subjects only fine-tuning public pretrained weights makes sense, compared in frozen folds; a token transformer fits stage 2 better (voxel-token-transformers-unproven-at-300-subjects-2026-10).

RegelScore an external model with exactly your own post-processing -- never let a threshold search reach 0vorschlag2026-11-01External probability maps: own component-size filter, threshold grid without 0; t=0.00 with sens 0.99 was a touch-rule artefact

Herkunft: Projekt mb-arena (Alita), external baselines job wk1 (SHIVA-CMB as downloaded on a clinical cohort), review 2026-11-01 · eigene Seite · Markdown

Aussage

An external CMB model (SHIVA-CMB, Tsuchida et al. 2024) was evaluated on a clinical cohort with a threshold chosen inside the training folds. The search picked t = 0.00 (probability > 0) on both sequences and reported sensitivity 0.99 with lesion F1 0.87, far above every detector so far; the agent called it "a real finding". It is a metric artefact: without the min/max component size of the own chain, p > 0 forms few huge components, and the touch rule (a reference lesion counts as found if any predicted voxel touches it) lets one blob "find" many CMB. As downloaded (t = 0.5) the same model gave a plausible, low result.

Traegt

Any comparison of an external probability map with an own detector: apply the same connected-component filter (minimum volume, maximum component size; here 2 mm3 and 2100 voxels at 0.25 mm3) before matching; search thresholds on a grid that excludes 0 and 1 (e.g. 0.05-0.95); report the component-size distribution at the chosen threshold; treat sensitivity

= 0.98 with high F1, or a threshold at the grid edge, as a bug until shown otherwise.

Traegt nicht

Models whose output is already a binary mask with their own post-processing -- there, score the mask as delivered and say so.

Bruchindikator

With identical post-processing and a grid without 0, the nested threshold still lands at the lowest grid value AND the component sizes stay CMB-like.

Ablesung

Own review 2026-11-01 (own-cohort numbers withheld; artefact described qualitatively). Model: Tsuchida et al. 2024, SHIVA-CMB, Scientific Reports, doi:10.1038/s41598-024-81870-5, PMID 39730628; code github.com/pboutinaud/SHIVA_CMB (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Correction job queued (wk1b).

RegelWhen a public detector over-detects at a new site, test "re-choose the threshold on the n local scans" before claiming a fine-tuning gainvorschlag2026-10-31Over-detecting public detector at a new site: compare every fine-tuning curve with "re-choose threshold/min size on the same n scans"; only the gain above that is from the weights

Herkunft: phase-2 design of the CMB adaptation study (valdo-t2s), reviewer check 2026-10-31 · eigene Seite · Markdown

Aussage

A public CMB U-Net applied unchanged to a new scanner typically keeps its sensitivity and floods the scan with false positives (own data: about 20x the false positives per scan of its own cross-validation; external example: CMB-HUNT, 0.54 -> 1.9 false positives per patient at similar sensitivity). Much of that is an operating-point shift, not missing knowledge: the same weights reach a far better F1 at a higher threshold. A fine-tuning curve F1(n) must therefore be compared with the cheapest adaptation that uses the same n annotated scans: keep the weights, re-choose threshold and minimum lesion size on the n scans, apply to the test fold. Report the test-optimal cell as the upper bound of any calibration. Only the part of the fine-tuning gain above this control is attributable to updating the weights.

Trägt

Reasoning plus the measured calibration shift of full-data models (linked finding); calibration as the one-parameter analogue of temperature scaling. AdaBN-type label-free normalisation adaptation is no alternative for instance-norm U-Nets (instance norm already uses per-scan statistics).

Trägt nicht

Not yet measured as a curve (queued 2026-10-31). A threshold chosen on 3 scans can be very noisy; report the draw spread.

Bruchindikator

The calibration-only curve stays at the zero-shot level for every n (then the gap is in the weights, not the threshold).

Ablesung

Guo et al., ICML 2017, "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks", arXiv:1706.04599. Suwalska et al., Comput Biol Med 2022, doi:10.1016/j.compbiomed.2022.106233, PMID 36370581 (CMB-HUNT, external FP rise). Yu et al., Radiol Artif Intell 2022, doi:10.1148/ryai.210064, PMID 35652114 (most externally validated imaging algorithms lose performance). Aussage

  1. Folds are data, not code. The 5-fold assignment of every cohort is written once (VALDO: stratified by cohort x burden, seed 0) and then frozen as a file with a SHA-256; every run input (candidate sets, prediction folders, manifests, synthetic copies) is checked against it before a run, and a different assignment is refused, never written over. On VALDO all inputs of Arena 1 and Arena 2 agreed (57 cases, 0 mismatches in two candidate sets, three runs and the manifest). Stage-2 GPU code runs with deterministic cuDNN kernels so that a rerun repeats.
  2. A new candidate source needs a cap before it meets patch extraction. Uncapped radial-symmetry and per-scan Gaussian-mixture sources produced 33,661-125,920 candidates on VALDO instead of 278 from the U-Net; storing a crop for each one took ~41 GB and the runner was killed by the out-of-memory killer three times. The ceiling gain was real (perfect-stage-2 F1 0.851 -> 0.92-0.95, all sources together 0.978), so the source is kept, but only the K strongest new components per scan (by radial-symmetry peak) are passed on, and its ceiling is measured on the maps alone (no crops) first.

Trägt

Own runs 2026-10-25 (VALDO check output; kernel OOM log; candidate counts from the ceiling tool).

Trägt nicht

The cap value K is not measured yet; the in-house fold check runs on the local agent.

Bruchindikator

A fold check that reports mismatches; a capped source whose candidate count still exceeds about twice the U-Net's.

Ablesung

Tools in the project: fold freeze/check (sha256), candidate-source cap (--max-new, --min-voxels). VALDO 2021 Task 2 data: Sudre CH et al. 2024, Medical Image Analysis, DOI 10.1016/j.media.2023.103029, PMID 37988921.

RegelExample scripts that restate a pipeline must reproduce its command bit for bitvorschlag2026-10-22Example scripts (train, predict, fine-tune) against the commands on the CPU with fixed seeds: weights equal with torch.equal, masks voxel for voxel; one comparison with a model that finds lesions; copies checked by source text (2026-10-22)

Herkunft: Python examples of cmb_3d_unet_experiments (branch python-examples, commit 53b21a0), own runs 2026-10-22 · eigene Seite · Markdown

Aussage

Readable example scripts (train, predict, fine-tune written out step by step) are only trustworthy if a test shows that they compute what the package's own commands compute. With fixed seeds on the CPU that is an exact test, not a tolerance:

  1. Weights equal with torch.equal, masks equal voxel for voxel. Measured 2026-10-22: 01_train_unet.py against cmbx train --frst_from cmbx, 03_fine_tune_unet.py against cmbx fine_tune --frst_from cmbx (same weights, same held-out cases, same threshold and minimum size), 02_predict_microbleeds.py against cmbx evaluate (same masks on the scan grid, same lesion counts).
  2. Equality needs the same order of random draws: seed torch, then the numpy generator, then build the network, then the optimizer over the same parameter list, then the patch draws in the same order. Predicting the held-out cases draws nothing, so the script may evaluate at other epochs without changing the weights.
  3. Two empty masks prove nothing. Keep one comparison with a model that finds lesions: the public VALDO model found 88 and 69 voxels on the two synthetic phantoms, and the test asserts a count above zero.
  4. Copies stay copies: scripts meant to be read alone repeat a few functions; a test compares their source text (inspect.getsource), so a fix in one cannot silently miss the other.
  5. Numbered top-level scripts with a process pool need the if __name__ == "__main__": guard around the steps (spawn and forkserver re-import the main module); functions stay above it, tests load the file with importlib.

Trägt

13 tests (about 90 s on 4 CPU threads, tiny network of width 8), full suite 322 green in a fresh environment. The same scripts gave the same numbers on a second run in a new environment (held-out F1 0.133 and 0.100 at epoch 1 and 2, identical to the command).

Trägt nicht

Bit equality holds for one machine and one PyTorch build on the CPU; on a GPU (bfloat16 autocast) and across releases only closeness can be expected (PyTorch documentation). The from-zero check ran on the development workstation: a new local environment, and a delegated "remote" agent that in fact ran on the same machine (GPU hidden, warm pip cache). A truly fresh CPU-only machine is still open; such a check must first record host name and GPU, otherwise it does not count as a fresh machine.

Bruchindikator

A seed-for-seed rerun of script and command on the CPU that gives different weights, or a predict comparison whose masks are all empty.

Ablesung

PyTorch, "Reproducibility" notes (https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/notes/randomness.html, checked 2026-10-22): completely reproducible results are not guaranteed across releases, commits or platforms, nor between CPU and GPU; torch.manual_seed seeds the generator for all devices, and with other nondeterminism removed the same environment gives the same numbers. Python, multiprocessing, "Safe importing of main module" (https://docs.python.org/3/library/multiprocessing.html, checked 2026-10-22): with spawn or forkserver the main module must import without side effects; protect the entry point with if __name__ == '__main__':; since Python 3.14 forkserver is the POSIX default. Code: https://github.com/mendeltem/cmb_3d_unet_experiments (examples/python, tests/test_python_examples.py). Own runs 2026-10-22 on synthetic phantoms only.

Herkunft: acceptance test of the inference of cmb_3d_unet_experiments, 2026-10-16 · eigene Seite · Markdown

Aussage

A detector that works on a fine grid (0.5 x 0.5 x 1 mm) and writes its mask back onto the scan grid (e.g. 0.7 x 0.7 x 5 mm slices) can merge two neighbouring microbleeds into one connected component of the binary mask: measured 37 separate detections on the model grid, 36 components after resampling (nearest neighbour in world coordinates). Microbleeds smaller than one input voxel can also vanish (kept here by setting their centre voxel). Rule: the count comes from the model grid; besides the binary mask, write a label map in which detection k has value k and matches entry k of the report (volume, probability, centre in mm and as input voxel).

Trägt

Acceptance test on real scans: json count = distinct values of the label map = model-grid count for every case after the change; before it, three of three checks on 5 mm data failed.

Trägt nicht

Says nothing about whether the merged pair is clinically one or two microbleeds; the model grid decides.

Bruchindikator

Counting components of a binary mask on thick slices and comparing them with a report.

Ablesung

Own measurement 2026-10-16 (5 mm T2* slices). Background on small-lesion counting in CMB detection: Sudre et al., Med Image Anal 2024, doi:10.1016/j.media.2023.103029, PMID 37988921 (VALDO challenge).

Aussage

1. The headroom sits in stage 2, and stage 2 is where new models can be measured. With a perfect candidate classifier our chain would reach about F1 0.85 on VALDO instead of about 0.65 (candidates reached at stage-1 threshold 0.15). The seed spread of stage 2 on fixed candidates is about 10x smaller than that of a stage-1 training (VALDO sd 0.009 against about 0.08 between single stage-1 seeds; in-house cohorts smaller still), and one stage-2 arm costs about one GPU hour. New models therefore go into stage 2 first; stage-1 changes need about 6 GPU hours per comparison and can only resolve effects of about 0.08.

2. Backbone swaps are exhausted, by our data and by the literature. 15 stage-1 networks lost to the anisotropic U-Net in our arena. CenSynCMB's own re-implementations on VALDO Task 2 give SwinUNETR F1 54.3 and VISTA3D 56.4 against 65.8 for an Attention U-Net backbone [1]; nnU-Net Revisited finds Mamba layers "have no effect" [14]; SAM-Med3D removed all training masks below 1 cm3 [15], so CMB-sized objects lie outside it. Our stage-1 logic (segmentation, connected components, max score) is the "DetU-Net" baseline of Retina U-Net: 3D LIDC mAP10 36.6 against 49.8, lost mainly through high-confidence false positives [6]; nnDetection made the Retina U-Net template safe for small objects [7]. A jointly trained, decoupled false-positive branch raised nodule detection by 10.27 % [8]. Top-K centre-point matching beats a single-centroid CenterNet by 21 points sensitivity at 1 FP/scan [9], a plausible reason why our centre-heatmap head failed. 3D group convolutions reach the FROC of a CNN trained on ten times more data (0.550 -> 0.767 at 300 training samples) [10]; our pooled classifier has about a hundred positive VALDO candidates. Counter-evidence: detection-head re-implementations score 33.6-61.0 on VALDO against 65.8 [1], so a detector is a test, not a promise.

Aussage

Question: can an MRI volume plus ROI be fed to a transformer as a flat 1D sequence (voxel = token, like an LLM), and does that help CMB detection with about 300 subjects?

  1. Feasible in principle, cost is the limit. Flattening loses no information if each token carries its (x,y,z) position embedding, or if the order follows a space-filling curve (Hilbert/Z-order) that keeps neighbours close. Global attention cost per head and layer (computed, n^2): - whole brain 1 mm (182x218x182 = 7.2e6 voxels) as voxel tokens: 5.2e13 pairs, infeasible; - crop 128^3 with patch 4^3: 32,768 tokens, 1.1e9 pairs, roughly the limit of dense attention; - crop 128^3 with patch 2^3: 262,144 tokens, 6.9e10 pairs global, but 9e7 with 7^3 window attention (Swin); - ROI-only tokens, e.g. 20,000 voxels at patch 1: 4e8 pairs, feasible. Halving the patch edge gives 8x tokens and 64x attention cost. A CMB of 2-10 mm spans 2-10 voxels at 1 mm, so a 16^3 patch swallows it; 4^3 or 2^3 resolves it.
  2. Evidence for small patches is thin. Only one preprint measures 3D patch size down to 1: ViT-Small fine-tuned on MedMNIST3D at 28^3, patch 1 vs 14 up to +23.78 % balanced accuracy, cost ~0.4 -> >800 GFLOPs per volume, no CNN baseline, one MRA set only (Dehghan et al. 2026, arXiv:2602.18614, preprint). Pixel-token transformers work on natural 2D images (Nguyen et al., ICLR 2025, peer-reviewed). No voxel-token or point-cloud model for brain lesions (WMH, CMB, stroke) with an nnU-Net baseline was found.
  3. Long-sequence alternatives exist but serialise features, not raw voxels: Perceiver / Perceiver IO (Jaegle et al. 2021/2022, arXiv:2103.03206, arXiv:2107.14795); Hilbert-curve Mamba on MRI (GAMBAS, arXiv:2504.04523, preprint). Mamba results depend on the serialisation order. PerceiverIO on brain MRI segmentation with k-space input: arXiv:2407.01367 (status unclear).
  4. Data size. Transformers lack the locality prior of CNNs and need more data (ViT below ResNet on ImageNet-1k, ahead only with 300 M images; Dosovitskiy et al., ICLR 2021, arXiv:2010.11929). With ~300 labelled subjects, training from scratch is not sensible. The large numbers in circulation are unlabelled pretraining sets, not a requirement for the fine-tuning set: - BrainSegFounder: SSL on 41,400 UK Biobank participants, Swin UNETR (Cox et al., Med Image Anal 2024, doi:10.1016/j.media.2024.103301, peer-reviewed); weights under the UK Biobank MTA (access needed). - AMAES / BRAINS-45K: 44,756 public brain MRI volumes, code, checkpoints and data open (Munk et al., arXiv:2408.00640, preprint). - FOMO60K: 60,529 scans from 11,187 subjects (scans are not subjects). - FOMO25 challenge: small pretrained models did well; larger models or longer training gave no reliable gain; MAE pretraining favours segmentation (arXiv:2604.11679, preprint).
  5. Counter-evidence in this wiki. On VALDO, Swin UNETR and other isotropic 3D nets lost against a U-Net whose first level convolves only in-plane (anisotropic-first-level-beats-isotropic-3d-nets), and no CMB-specific transformer or foundation model was retrieved (literature-2023-2026-shiva-cmb-is-the-public-comparable). Pretraining sets are mostly T1/FLAIR; whether they help SWI/T2* magnitude is open.

Aussage

A literature review of automatic CMB detection on SWI/T2* (PubMed + Europe PMC, 37 retrieved records; arXiv not reachable, HTTP 429 through the proxy) gives three actionable results for this project:

  1. The closest published analogue of our chain is SHIVA-CMB. One 3D U-Net trained once on 450 scans from 7 acquisitions and 6 cohorts, T2 and SWI mixed (Tsuchida 2024, PMID 39730628): held-out test sensitivity 0.67, precision 0.82, F1 (Dice) 0.74, 0.6 false positives per scan; on an evaluation-only set with acquisitions never seen in training 0.67 / 0.91 / 0.77 / 0.5 FP; correlation with expert visual counts in 1,992 T2-weighted scans Pearson R 0.89. Both the pipeline (SHiVAi) and the pretrained weights (SHIVA-CMB) are public -- a free external baseline we should run on our data.
  2. Precision, not sensitivity, separates the methods. The best lesion-level numbers reported 2023-2026 come from pipelines whose candidate stage is deliberately constrained: morphology filter bank + CNN, 95.0 % sensitivity at 4.37 FP/patient versus 97.5 % at 25.87 FP without the filter bank (Nishioka 2025, PMID 38494702); a geometric-to-neural cascade (GMM candidate generation + two 3D ResNets on QA labels) removing 76.8 % of stage-A false positives, F1 0.693, AUC 0.9587 (Bogdanov 2026 preprint, DOI 10.64898/2026.07.24.740624); U-Net with CBAM attention, large kernels and anatomy localisation, F1 0.887 at 0.83 FP/scan on 506 multi-site scans (Cho 2026, PMID 41859233). This matches our own negative finding that sensitivity levers do not raise lesion F1.
  3. Mimic handling is a contrast problem, not an architecture problem. QSM separates CMB from calcification better than SWI/phase on accuracy, confidence and inter-rater agreement (Kang 2025, PMID 39690177); adding a high-pass filtered phase channel to SWI gave 95.8 % sensitivity, 70.9 % precision, 1.6 FP/case (Liu 2019, PMID 31121296); multi-modal T2w/SWI/QSM input improved CMB-versus-iron separation, QSM being the most informative channel (Rashid 2021, PMID 34238951). Anatomical masking (ventricles/CSF/choroid plexus/cerebellum/edges) is a free false-positive filter present in the top pipelines -- consistent with our CSF rule.

Secondary results: synthetic CMBs are now a first-class training strategy (analytic model, 37,000 lesions released, ~9 FP/scan when trained on synthetic only, Momeni 2021, PMID 34051412; 3D lesion GAN transferring to unseen MRI parameters, PMID 34975381; self-supervised pseudo-labels giving +46 % average precision on ex-vivo T2, Nikseresht 2026, PMID 42107619). The SWI/T2 domain gap is measurable: synthesising SWI from T2 magnitude recovers 19/28 CMBs seen on real SWI versus 10/28 on magnitude images (Genc 2023, PMID 36733222). Meta-analytic pooled DL accuracy is high (lesion-level sensitivity 0.96 / specificity 0.98, patient-level 0.89 / 0.86, Feng 2026, PMID 42767630), but the authors themselves warn the study count is small. SWI detects about 1.6x more CMBs than T2-GRE (13-study meta-analysis, Yang 2026, PMID 41775374), so sequence mix dominates any single F1 number.

  • MicrobleedNet (knowledge distillation, 3-step) reported cluster-wise TPR >90% with <2 false positives per subject and TPR >80% across datasets [1]; VALDO 2021 found 9 microbleed teams but large inter-team and inter-case variability and no practically useful result for lacunes [2]. These two anchors still define the field's reference numbers.
  • The dominant 2023–2026 architecture is unchanged in spirit: a 3D U-Net or 3D CNN detector, often with a second stage (classifier / refinement) for false-positive reduction [3,4,5,6,7,8]. What is new is the detail: attention-gated skip connections, region-proposal heads, hard-sample/prototype learning, triplanar ensembling, morphology filter banks, and geometric cascade candidate generation.
  • Lesion-level scores have crept up: e.g. precision 0.891 / recall 0.887 / F1 0.887 with 0.83 FP/scan on 506 multi-site scans [3]; a geometric-to-neural cascade reached F1 0.693 while removing 76.8% of stage-A false positives [8]; sensitivity ~96% at low FP for a single-stage triplanar ensemble [4] (unverified); ~95% at 4.37 FP/patient for a morphology-filter-bank + CNN on 2D T2* [6].
  • The most directly comparable public tool to our setup is SHIVA-CMB: one 3D U-Net trained once on 450 scans from 7 acquisitions / 6 cohorts (T2 and SWI mixed), test sensitivity 0.67, precision 0.82, F1(Dice) 0.74*, 0.6 FP/scan; on an unseen-acquisition set 0.67 / 0.91 / 0.77 / 0.5 FP — and it is publicly released (pipeline + weights) [9].
  • Domain shift SWI ↔ T2 is now addressed explicitly rather than ignored: cross-modality generalisation is reported as a headline result [3], synthetic-SWI-from-T2 synthesis recovers CMBs that are invisible on magnitude images (19/28 vs 10/28) [10], and zero-shot super-resolution of thick-slice SWI improves downstream CMB segmentation [11].
  • Synthetic data has become a first-class strategy: an analytic synthetic-CMB model with 37,000 generated lesions trains a detector to near state of the art (~9 FP/scan) using synthetic data only [12]; a 3D lesion GAN conditioned on volume/location transfers to unseen datasets with different MRI parameters [13]; self-supervised pretraining on ex-vivo T2* with fuzzy-segmentation pseudo-labels reached 90% of definite CMBs at 15 FP/scan [14].
  • Mimics remain the central problem and are handled mainly with extra contrast (high-pass filtered phase, QSM) and anatomical constraints rather than with new network families: QSM separates CMB from calcification better than SWI/phase [15]; a two-channel SWI + phase model reached sensitivity 95.8%, precision 70.9%, 1.6 FP/case [16]; QSM improved CMB-vs-iron separation (sensitivity 0.84–0.88, precision 0.40–0.59) [17].
  • Transformers and foundation models exist but are not yet CMB-specific in the retrieved literature: the retrieved foundation-model work is a universal brain-MRI lesion-segmentation model with a Mixture-of-Modality- Experts design evaluated on 8 lesion types (not CMB) [18], plus reviews of vision-language foundation models [19] and diffusion models [20] that list open problems (prompt dependence, mask hallucination, slow volumetric inference, limited annotation, domain-shift sensitivity) but report no CMB detection results.
  • External/cross-cohort validation is better than in 2021 but still the exception: SHIVA-CMB validates on unseen acquisitions and correlates with expert counts in 1,992 scans (R=0.89) [9]; nnU-Net was validated on independent internal and external sets [21]; other papers validate within one or two cohorts only [3,4,5].
  • Pooled meta-analytic DL accuracy looks high (lesion level sensitivity 0.96 / specificity 0.98; patient level 0.89 / 0.86) but the authors themselves flag the small number of studies and call for multicentre work [22]; SWI detects ~1.6× more CMBs than T2*-GRE, so sequence choice dominates any model comparison [23].

Practical recommendation (as written for our setup)

Setup: 3D U-Net per dataset plus an optional second-stage classifier; pooled lesion F1 (own number kept outside the wiki) on in-house T2*/SWI. The retrieved evidence maps onto this as follows.

  1. Our pooled F1 is in the expected band, but the comparison that matters is not published. SHIVA-CMB — one 3D U-Net across seven acquisitions and two modalities, i.e. the closest published analogue of "train once, apply to our data" — reports F1/Dice 0.74 (0.77 on unseen acquisitions) [9]. A per-dataset U-Net pooling below that is plausible but sits at the lower edge; the gap is most likely in false positives (mimics) rather than in sensitivity [3,6,8,16]. Report precision, recall and FP per scan separately, never F1 alone, because F1 hides exactly the trade-off that differs between datasets.
  2. Keep the two-stage design; invest in stage two. The retrieved false-positive wins come from the second stage: a 3D CNN discriminator on top of a 3D U-Net [7], a geometric cascade's second ResNet removing 76.8% of stage-A FPs [8], a morphology filter bank cutting 25.87→4.37 FP/patient [6], and knowledge distillation lifting cluster-wise TPR by 15% [1]. For our pipeline, an explicit stage-2 classifier trained on hard negatives (vessel cross-sections, calcification, iron, choroid plexus, image edges) is the single highest-value change — exactly the "optional second-stage classifier" we already have, but it should be trained deliberately on mimics.
  3. Add phase and/or QSM channels where available. The retrieved mimic literature is unambiguous: QSM separates CMB from calcification better than SWI/phase [15], and multi-modal T2w/SWI/QSM input improves CMB-vs-iron separation [17]. Adding a high-pass filtered phase channel is the cheapest step (sensitivity 95.8%, precision 70.9%, 1.6 FP/case reported with SWI+phase [16]). This attacks precision, our likely weak point.
  4. Use anatomical masking. Masking ventricles/CSF/choroid plexus/cerebellum/edges is cheap and was part of the top-performing pipelines [1,4,8]. It costs nothing at inference and requires no labels.
  5. Attack the T2*/SWI domain gap explicitly, since we train per dataset. Options in increasing effort: (a) train one model on both modalities and evaluate cross-modality as a first-class metric, as RLK-UNet [3] and SHIVA-CMB [9] do; (b) synthesise SWI from T2 magnitude with a DeepSWI-style network to normalise contrast [10]; (c) zero-shot super-resolution for thick-slice SWI before detection [11,30]. If per-dataset training is kept, at minimum report cross-dataset (SWI-model on T2 and vice versa) numbers so the domain gap is quantified.
  6. Use synthetic CMBs for augmentation and pre-training. The analytic 37,000-lesion synthetic set is released and reached ~9 FP/scan when training on synthetic only [12]; a 3D lesion GAN transfers to unseen datasets and different MRI parameters [13]; self-supervised pretraining with pseudo-labels lifted average precision by 46% over a real-data-only baseline in a low-prevalence, mimic-rich setting [14]. The GAN/synthetic route is particularly attractive for our per-dataset setup, where each dataset has few labelled lesions.
  7. Benchmark against SHIVA-CMB before building anything new. It is a public 3D U-Net trained across T2*/SWI cohorts with released weights [9]. Running it on our data gives a free, published external baseline and immediately shows whether our per-dataset F1 is a data problem or a pipeline problem.
  8. Do not expect transformers/foundation models to help yet. The retrieved foundation-model work targets generic brain lesion segmentation, not CMB [18], and the reviews list open problems (prompt dependence, mask hallucination, slow volumetric inference, domain-shift sensitivity, limited annotation) without CMB numbers [19,20]. Transformers are a research direction here, not a practical upgrade.
  9. Reporting discipline. Given VALDO's "performance inconsistency across cases" [2] and the meta-analysis's call for multicentre validation [22], report at minimum: lesion-level precision/recall/F1, FP per scan, cluster- wise detection at a fixed FP budget, and a subject-level count correlation — the last one is what SHIVA-CMB used to validate against expert reads in 1,992 scans (R=0.89) [9], and it is what clinical users actually need.

Trägt nicht

Abstract-level evidence only (no full texts read); a literature map, not a systematic review. Open questions:

  • No CMB-specific transformer or foundation model was retrieved. Whether vision-language or MoME-style foundation models transfer to 3–10 mm sparse lesions like CMBs is unverified [18,19].
  • Standardisation of metrics. "Cluster-wise TPR", Dice, lesion-level F1 and per-patient sensitivity are used interchangeably across the retrieved literature; no retrieved work provides a head-to-head comparison of the same models on the same data under one metric [1,3,9,22].
  • How much of the SWI-vs-T2* gap is contrast vs resolution? Super-resolution [11,30] and synthesis [10] both help, but no retrieved study decomposes the two effects.
  • Synthetic-data fidelity. Both the analytic model [12] and the GAN [13] claim transfer to unseen datasets, but neither reports an external lesion-level precision on a fully unseen modality (the GAN reports unseen MRI parameters, not unseen modality).
  • Post-2021 VALDO follow-up. No retrieved work is a successor benchmark to VALDO; the challenge's weak-label and case-level-inconsistency findings [2] have not been resolved by a new consensus evaluation.
  • ARIA-H / anti-amyloid context. RLK-UNet targets ARIA-H severity categories [3] and accelerated SWI is being evaluated for ARIA monitoring [36]; whether current detectors meet the regulatory bar for therapy monitoring is unaddressed.
  • arXiv gap in this report. Foundation-model and transformer preprints were not retrievable (proxy returned HTTP 429 / Rate exceeded. throughout); the foundation-model picture here is therefore limited to the peer-reviewed work that was retrieved [18,19,20].

Ein Netz, das nur dicke Schichten kennt, versagt auf duennen; eines, das nur duenne und mittlere kennt, ueberdetektiert auf dicken (32 Fehlalarme je Fall). Traegt

Planung von Trainingsdaten fuer Laesionsdetektion: je Zielgeometrie (Schichtdicke, Aufloesung) muessen Faelle im Training sein; "mehr Daten" aus anderen Scannern sind kein Ersatz. Vor dem Einsatz auf neuen Daten: Schichtdicke pruefen und das Modell nehmen, dessen Training sie enthaelt.

Traegt nicht

Aussagen ueber Sequenzen (T2* gegen SWI) -- dort ist der Verlust noch groesser und schon bekannt; und ueber Startwert-Rauschen: ein Seed, zweiter Seed und Ensemble folgen (Freitags-Warteschlange).

Ein zweiter Seed oder ein Ensemble, bei dem die 0,8-mm-Kohorte ohne sich selbst auf das Niveau der Faltenmodelle kommt (Differenz unter 0,08).

Aussage

<sub>_space-T2S_desc-masked_T1.nii.gz von VALDO 2021 Task 2 enthaelt bei 33 von 72 Faellen NaN: sub-101 bis 106 rund 23 % der Voxel, sub-301 bis 327 (alle 27) 34-38 %. _T2S und _CMB sind bei allen Faellen sauber; in Kohorte 3 traegt zusaetzlich _T2 NaN. Es sind die GELIEFERTEN Dateien.

Teuer ist der lautlose Ausfall: NaN vergiftet die Perzentilnormierung von mri_synthseg, das Netz gibt ueberall Hintergrund aus, Rueckgabewert 0, Ausgabedatei vorhanden. Erst die nachgelagerte Rasterumrechnung faellt auf ("nur 1 Label"), und der Fall wird verworfen; die Rasterumrechnung stand zwei Stunden zu Unrecht unter Verdacht. Kein Gegenbeispiel in beide Richtungen: jeder NaN-Fall scheitert, jeder saubere liefert 33 Labels in 20-22 s.

Die Reparatur ist eine Zeile im Ladeschritt und geprueft sicher: np.nan_to_num(..., nan=0.0). Die NaN bilden eine Randschale vom Umrastern ausserhalb des Quellbilds (eine zusammenhaengende Komponente am Volumenrand, nach dreifacher Erosion der Hirnmaske kein NaN im Inneren); Nullsetzen erfindet keine Anatomie. An echten Daten bestaetigt: sub-304 von 1 Label / 0 Voxel auf 33 Labels / 1,65 M Voxel. Gelieferte Dateien nicht aendern.

Zwei Fallen beim Nachbauen: der Segmentierer leitet den AUSGABEnamen aus dem EINGABEnamen ab -- eine bereinigte Kopie erzeugt eine zweite Ausgabe neben der alten leeren, und ein Muster ohne sorted erwischt die alte. Und er ueberspringt vorhandene Ausgaben nur bei GLEICHEM Namen.

Traegt

Jede Kette, die VALDO-T1 nutzt (SynthSeg, Registrierung, Mehrkanal-Eingabe). Allgemein: bei jedem neuen Bilddatensatz einmal np.isnan(...).sum() je Modalitaet zaehlen, bevor eine Kette laeuft.

Traegt nicht

Ketten, die nur T2* und CMB-Referenz lesen; dort sind die Daten sauber.

Bruchindikator

Eine korrigierte VALDO-Fassung ohne NaN.

Ablesung

Eigene Zaehlung ueber alle 72 Faelle (Sudre et al. 2024, doi:10.1016/j.media.2023.103029). Tier 1a.

Aussage

MicrobleedNet (Sundaresan et al. 2023) uebermalt vor dem Netz alles, was ein Frangi-Gefaessfilter je Schicht findet, und rettet nur, was rund und kompakt aussieht. Auf VALDO (0,8 bis 4 mm Schichten) trifft diese Uebermalung 109 von 236 annotierten Blutungen, uebermalt 27 vollstaendig und macht 17 unsichtbar; sie malt 12 bis 17 Prozent des Hirns neu. Training ohne die Uebermalung: F1 0,470 -> 0,588, gepaart +0,136 [+0,065; +0,226] -- der groesste Einzelgewinn der ganzen Kette. Eine reparierte, labelfreie Uebermalung (Skalen in mm, eine Schwelle je Volumen, nur lange duenne Strukturen) brachte in drei Dosen nichts (0,588 / 0,584 / 0,570).

Traegt

Jede Wiederverwendung von MicrobleedNet oder aehnlicher "Gefaesse zuerst entfernen"- Vorverarbeitung auf Daten mit Schichten >= 2 mm. Die Autoren nennen das Risiko selbst; ihr Verfahren entstand auf duennschichtigem SWI.

Traegt nicht

Duennschichtiges, isotropes SWI. Dort wurde der Schaden hier nicht gemessen.

Bruchindikator

Ein Datensatz mit dicken Schichten, auf dem die Uebermalung gepaart gewinnt.
