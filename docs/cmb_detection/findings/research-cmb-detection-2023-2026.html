Literature: automatic cerebral microbleed detection 2023-2026 compared with MicrobleedNet and VALDO

Aussage

  • MicrobleedNet (knowledge distillation, 3-step) reported cluster-wise TPR >90% with <2 false positives per subject and TPR >80% across datasets [1]; VALDO 2021 found 9 microbleed teams but large inter-team and inter-case variability and no practically useful result for lacunes [2]. These two anchors still define the field's reference numbers.
  • The dominant 2023–2026 architecture is unchanged in spirit: a 3D U-Net or 3D CNN detector, often with a second stage (classifier / refinement) for false-positive reduction [3,4,5,6,7,8]. What is new is the detail: attention-gated skip connections, region-proposal heads, hard-sample/prototype learning, triplanar ensembling, morphology filter banks, and geometric cascade candidate generation.
  • Lesion-level scores have crept up: e.g. precision 0.891 / recall 0.887 / F1 0.887 with 0.83 FP/scan on 506 multi-site scans [3]; a geometric-to-neural cascade reached F1 0.693 while removing 76.8% of stage-A false positives [8]; sensitivity ~96% at low FP for a single-stage triplanar ensemble [4] (unverified); ~95% at 4.37 FP/patient for a morphology-filter-bank + CNN on 2D T2* [6].
  • The most directly comparable public tool to our setup is SHIVA-CMB: one 3D U-Net trained once on 450 scans from 7 acquisitions / 6 cohorts (T2 and SWI mixed), test sensitivity 0.67, precision 0.82, F1(Dice) 0.74*, 0.6 FP/scan; on an unseen-acquisition set 0.67 / 0.91 / 0.77 / 0.5 FP — and it is publicly released (pipeline + weights) [9].
  • Domain shift SWI ↔ T2 is now addressed explicitly rather than ignored: cross-modality generalisation is reported as a headline result [3], synthetic-SWI-from-T2 synthesis recovers CMBs that are invisible on magnitude images (19/28 vs 10/28) [10], and zero-shot super-resolution of thick-slice SWI improves downstream CMB segmentation [11].
  • Synthetic data has become a first-class strategy: an analytic synthetic-CMB model with 37,000 generated lesions trains a detector to near state of the art (~9 FP/scan) using synthetic data only [12]; a 3D lesion GAN conditioned on volume/location transfers to unseen datasets with different MRI parameters [13]; self-supervised pretraining on ex-vivo T2* with fuzzy-segmentation pseudo-labels reached 90% of definite CMBs at 15 FP/scan [14].
  • Mimics remain the central problem and are handled mainly with extra contrast (high-pass filtered phase, QSM) and anatomical constraints rather than with new network families: QSM separates CMB from calcification better than SWI/phase [15]; a two-channel SWI + phase model reached sensitivity 95.8%, precision 70.9%, 1.6 FP/case [16]; QSM improved CMB-vs-iron separation (sensitivity 0.84–0.88, precision 0.40–0.59) [17].
  • Transformers and foundation models exist but are not yet CMB-specific in the retrieved literature: the retrieved foundation-model work is a universal brain-MRI lesion-segmentation model with a Mixture-of-Modality- Experts design evaluated on 8 lesion types (not CMB) [18], plus reviews of vision-language foundation models [19] and diffusion models [20] that list open problems (prompt dependence, mask hallucination, slow volumetric inference, limited annotation, domain-shift sensitivity) but report no CMB detection results.
  • External/cross-cohort validation is better than in 2021 but still the exception: SHIVA-CMB validates on unseen acquisitions and correlates with expert counts in 1,992 scans (R=0.89) [9]; nnU-Net was validated on independent internal and external sets [21]; other papers validate within one or two cohorts only [3,4,5].
  • Pooled meta-analytic DL accuracy looks high (lesion level sensitivity 0.96 / specificity 0.98; patient level 0.89 / 0.86) but the authors themselves flag the small number of studies and call for multicentre work [22]; SWI detects ~1.6× more CMBs than T2*-GRE, so sequence choice dominates any model comparison [23].

Practical recommendation (as written for our setup)

Setup: 3D U-Net per dataset plus an optional second-stage classifier; pooled lesion F1 (own number kept outside the wiki) on in-house T2*/SWI. The retrieved evidence maps onto this as follows.

  1. Our pooled F1 is in the expected band, but the comparison that matters is not published. SHIVA-CMB — one 3D U-Net across seven acquisitions and two modalities, i.e. the closest published analogue of "train once, apply to our data" — reports F1/Dice 0.74 (0.77 on unseen acquisitions) [9]. A per-dataset U-Net pooling below that is plausible but sits at the lower edge; the gap is most likely in false positives (mimics) rather than in sensitivity [3,6,8,16]. Report precision, recall and FP per scan separately, never F1 alone, because F1 hides exactly the trade-off that differs between datasets.
  2. Keep the two-stage design; invest in stage two. The retrieved false-positive wins come from the second stage: a 3D CNN discriminator on top of a 3D U-Net [7], a geometric cascade's second ResNet removing 76.8% of stage-A FPs [8], a morphology filter bank cutting 25.87→4.37 FP/patient [6], and knowledge distillation lifting cluster-wise TPR by 15% [1]. For our pipeline, an explicit stage-2 classifier trained on hard negatives (vessel cross-sections, calcification, iron, choroid plexus, image edges) is the single highest-value change — exactly the "optional second-stage classifier" we already have, but it should be trained deliberately on mimics.
  3. Add phase and/or QSM channels where available. The retrieved mimic literature is unambiguous: QSM separates CMB from calcification better than SWI/phase [15], and multi-modal T2w/SWI/QSM input improves CMB-vs-iron separation [17]. Adding a high-pass filtered phase channel is the cheapest step (sensitivity 95.8%, precision 70.9%, 1.6 FP/case reported with SWI+phase [16]). This attacks precision, our likely weak point.
  4. Use anatomical masking. Masking ventricles/CSF/choroid plexus/cerebellum/edges is cheap and was part of the top-performing pipelines [1,4,8]. It costs nothing at inference and requires no labels.
  5. Attack the T2*/SWI domain gap explicitly, since we train per dataset. Options in increasing effort: (a) train one model on both modalities and evaluate cross-modality as a first-class metric, as RLK-UNet [3] and SHIVA-CMB [9] do; (b) synthesise SWI from T2 magnitude with a DeepSWI-style network to normalise contrast [10]; (c) zero-shot super-resolution for thick-slice SWI before detection [11,30]. If per-dataset training is kept, at minimum report cross-dataset (SWI-model on T2 and vice versa) numbers so the domain gap is quantified.
  6. Use synthetic CMBs for augmentation and pre-training. The analytic 37,000-lesion synthetic set is released and reached ~9 FP/scan when training on synthetic only [12]; a 3D lesion GAN transfers to unseen datasets and different MRI parameters [13]; self-supervised pretraining with pseudo-labels lifted average precision by 46% over a real-data-only baseline in a low-prevalence, mimic-rich setting [14]. The GAN/synthetic route is particularly attractive for our per-dataset setup, where each dataset has few labelled lesions.
  7. Benchmark against SHIVA-CMB before building anything new. It is a public 3D U-Net trained across T2*/SWI cohorts with released weights [9]. Running it on our data gives a free, published external baseline and immediately shows whether our per-dataset F1 is a data problem or a pipeline problem.
  8. Do not expect transformers/foundation models to help yet. The retrieved foundation-model work targets generic brain lesion segmentation, not CMB [18], and the reviews list open problems (prompt dependence, mask hallucination, slow volumetric inference, domain-shift sensitivity, limited annotation) without CMB numbers [19,20]. Transformers are a research direction here, not a practical upgrade.
  9. Reporting discipline. Given VALDO's "performance inconsistency across cases" [2] and the meta-analysis's call for multicentre validation [22], report at minimum: lesion-level precision/recall/F1, FP per scan, cluster- wise detection at a fixed FP budget, and a subject-level count correlation — the last one is what SHIVA-CMB used to validate against expert reads in 1,992 scans (R=0.89) [9], and it is what clinical users actually need.

Trägt nicht

Abstract-level evidence only (no full texts read); a literature map, not a systematic review. Open questions:

  • No CMB-specific transformer or foundation model was retrieved. Whether vision-language or MoME-style foundation models transfer to 3–10 mm sparse lesions like CMBs is unverified [18,19].
  • Standardisation of metrics. "Cluster-wise TPR", Dice, lesion-level F1 and per-patient sensitivity are used interchangeably across the retrieved literature; no retrieved work provides a head-to-head comparison of the same models on the same data under one metric [1,3,9,22].
  • How much of the SWI-vs-T2* gap is contrast vs resolution? Super-resolution [11,30] and synthesis [10] both help, but no retrieved study decomposes the two effects.
  • Synthetic-data fidelity. Both the analytic model [12] and the GAN [13] claim transfer to unseen datasets, but neither reports an external lesion-level precision on a fully unseen modality (the GAN reports unseen MRI parameters, not unseen modality).
  • Post-2021 VALDO follow-up. No retrieved work is a successor benchmark to VALDO; the challenge's weak-label and case-level-inconsistency findings [2] have not been resolved by a new consensus evaluation.
  • ARIA-H / anti-amyloid context. RLK-UNet targets ARIA-H severity categories [3] and accelerated SWI is being evaluated for ARIA monitoring [36]; whether current detectors meet the regulatory bar for therapy monitoring is unaddressed.
  • arXiv gap in this report. Foundation-model and transformer preprints were not retrievable (proxy returned HTTP 429 / Rate exceeded. throughout); the foundation-model picture here is therefore limited to the peer-reviewed work that was retrieved [18,19,20].

Claims not supported by the abstracts

The following claim in sections 2–4 is marked "(unverified)" in place because the abstract of the reference it cites does not support it (the substance may be supported by another reference's abstract, but the citation as written is not):

  • Section 2 (lesion-level scores): "sensitivity ~96% at low FP for a single-stage triplanar ensemble [4] (unverified)". The abstract of [4] (Kim et al. 2025) describes a single end-to-end 3D U-Net with a Region Proposal Network on SWI + phase and reports a sensitivity of 94.66% (vs 93.33% for the baseline RPN) — it is not a triplanar ensemble and does not report ~96%. The "triplanar ensemble" with 96.05% sensitivity is reference [27] (TPE-Det). The number itself is therefore supported by [27]'s abstract, but not by [4]'s, so the claim as cited by [4] is marked unverified.
