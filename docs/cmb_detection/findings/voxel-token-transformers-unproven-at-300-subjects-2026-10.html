Aussage

Question: can an MRI volume plus ROI be fed to a transformer as a flat 1D sequence (voxel = token, like an LLM), and does that help CMB detection with about 300 subjects?

  1. Feasible in principle, cost is the limit. Flattening loses no information if each token carries its (x,y,z) position embedding, or if the order follows a space-filling curve (Hilbert/Z-order) that keeps neighbours close. Global attention cost per head and layer (computed, n^2): - whole brain 1 mm (182x218x182 = 7.2e6 voxels) as voxel tokens: 5.2e13 pairs, infeasible; - crop 128^3 with patch 4^3: 32,768 tokens, 1.1e9 pairs, roughly the limit of dense attention; - crop 128^3 with patch 2^3: 262,144 tokens, 6.9e10 pairs global, but 9e7 with 7^3 window attention (Swin); - ROI-only tokens, e.g. 20,000 voxels at patch 1: 4e8 pairs, feasible. Halving the patch edge gives 8x tokens and 64x attention cost. A CMB of 2-10 mm spans 2-10 voxels at 1 mm, so a 16^3 patch swallows it; 4^3 or 2^3 resolves it.
  2. Evidence for small patches is thin. Only one preprint measures 3D patch size down to 1: ViT-Small fine-tuned on MedMNIST3D at 28^3, patch 1 vs 14 up to +23.78 % balanced accuracy, cost ~0.4 -> >800 GFLOPs per volume, no CNN baseline, one MRA set only (Dehghan et al. 2026, arXiv:2602.18614, preprint). Pixel-token transformers work on natural 2D images (Nguyen et al., ICLR 2025, peer-reviewed). No voxel-token or point-cloud model for brain lesions (WMH, CMB, stroke) with an nnU-Net baseline was found.
  3. Long-sequence alternatives exist but serialise features, not raw voxels: Perceiver / Perceiver IO (Jaegle et al. 2021/2022, arXiv:2103.03206, arXiv:2107.14795); Hilbert-curve Mamba on MRI (GAMBAS, arXiv:2504.04523, preprint). Mamba results depend on the serialisation order. PerceiverIO on brain MRI segmentation with k-space input: arXiv:2407.01367 (status unclear).
  4. Data size. Transformers lack the locality prior of CNNs and need more data (ViT below ResNet on ImageNet-1k, ahead only with 300 M images; Dosovitskiy et al., ICLR 2021, arXiv:2010.11929). With ~300 labelled subjects, training from scratch is not sensible. The large numbers in circulation are unlabelled pretraining sets, not a requirement for the fine-tuning set: - BrainSegFounder: SSL on 41,400 UK Biobank participants, Swin UNETR (Cox et al., Med Image Anal 2024, doi:10.1016/j.media.2024.103301, peer-reviewed); weights under the UK Biobank MTA (access needed). - AMAES / BRAINS-45K: 44,756 public brain MRI volumes, code, checkpoints and data open (Munk et al., arXiv:2408.00640, preprint). - FOMO60K: 60,529 scans from 11,187 subjects (scans are not subjects). - FOMO25 challenge: small pretrained models did well; larger models or longer training gave no reliable gain; MAE pretraining favours segmentation (arXiv:2604.11679, preprint).
  5. Counter-evidence in this wiki. On VALDO, Swin UNETR and other isotropic 3D nets lost against a U-Net whose first level convolves only in-plane (anisotropic-first-level-beats-isotropic-3d-nets), and no CMB-specific transformer or foundation model was retrieved (literature-2023-2026-shiva-cmb-is-the-public-comparable). Pretraining sets are mostly T1/FLAIR; whether they help SWI/T2* magnitude is open.

Trägt

Decision on whether to try a transformer for CMB detection: only as fine-tuning of public pretrained weights, compared in the same frozen folds against the current chain (anisotropic 3D U-Net plus stage-2 classifier), reported as lesion F1 and FP per scan, with two seeds (effects below ~0.08 F1 are seed noise). A transformer over ROI or candidate tokens fits stage 2 better than stage 1 (compare TrackSet-CMB in search-space-expansion-and-novel-hybrids-2026-10).

Trägt nicht

Any claim that voxel-token transformers beat CNNs on CMB; any minimum subject count for fine-tuning (none found); transfer of the MedMNIST 28^3 result to clinical resolution.

Bruchindikator

A peer-reviewed voxel-token or small-patch transformer that beats nnU-Net or the anisotropic U-Net on CMB lesion F1; a pretraining set with SWI/T2* where fine-tuned weights beat the chain in the same folds.
