Scope: a keyword audit of cmb_detection and lesion_detection_method found zero entries on semi-supervised learning, tabular / boosting candidate classifiers and frozen foundation-model features. Everything else asked for ("what can be improved") is already covered by rounds 1-3 and the search-space expansion. All three levers below are stage-2 arms (seed noise ~0.01, ~1 GPU hour or less), consistent with where the headroom is.

Aussage

1. Unlabelled scans: small gains at our label count, more likely a variance than a mean effect. No semi-supervised CMB paper on in-vivo SWI/T2* exists (PubMed query on microbleed AND semi-supervised / pseudo-label / self-training / self-supervised / unlabeled / mean teacher: one hit, ex-vivo). The one CMB result: self-supervised pretraining in an ex-vivo multi-echo GRE detector added about +0.007 AP (0.333 -> 0.340, 5 folds x 4 seeds) [1]; the paper's headline 46 % AP gain comes mainly from its synthesis, not from the pretraining. Analogues: - lung nodules, FocalMix: CPM +11.5 at 25 labelled scans, +4.4 at 100, +0.7 at 533 (no seeds) [2]; - brain metastases < 15 mm, noisy student with 217 labelled / 1,247 unlabelled: FP per scan at 90 % sensitivity 9.23 -> 8.44, at 80 % no change; looser pseudo-label thresholds made the student WORSE than the teacher [3]; - perivascular spaces, pseudo-labels on 12,740 unlabelled T1w: Dice 85.7 -> 85.6, a null result (preprint) [4]; - in-domain self-supervised pretraining for nodule FP reduction: AUC 96.03 +- 1.82 -> 98.34 +- 0.44 over 10 runs, i.e. the run-to-run spread fell about 4-fold [5]. Pattern: large gains only below ~50 labelled scans. Expected here (extrapolation, not measured): stage 1 +0.00 to +0.05 F1, below its seed noise; stage 2 +0.00 to +0.02.

2. A tabular stage 2 is a mandatory cheap ablation, not a long shot. No paper compares a CNN and a boosting / random-forest stage 2 on the SAME CMB candidates; the "CNN beats handcrafted" claims in CMB compare across datasets. Classical CMB pipelines: random-forest cascade on LoG candidates with Radon and Hessian shape descriptors, 87 % sensitivity at 27.1 FP per subject [6]; two-stage RF (voxel then object) on SWI, object RF improved every FROC point, remaining FPs were vessel segments that look spherical [7]; SVM on shape and intensity, 81.7 % (semi-automated) [8]. The only same-candidate head-to-head found (fundus red lesions, 28 and 78 training images, 407 positives) went to the tabular model, and the combination won [9]:

Counter-case: with ~1,000 positives on LUNA16 all top FP-reduction entries were CNNs (CPM 0.758-0.908 vs classical full systems 0.608-0.742) [10]. Our pool has about a hundred VALDO positives, i.e. the regime where [9] found the tabular model ahead. Feature list per candidate (first pass): volume, equivalent diameter, slices spanned, max in-plane area; PCA eigenvalue ratios (elongation), sphericity; multi-scale Hessian eigenvalues, Frangi vesselness and blobness at sigma 0.5-2 mm (anisotropy-aware); contrast to a surrounding shell, min / mean intensity; stage-1 max and mean probability; a tube score (how far the dark signal continues beyond the component along its main axis, a hand-made proxy of TrackSet's slice-track module); plus the round-3 features (vote count, edge distance, vein atlas).

3. Frozen foundation features: only with patch-local pooling at the candidate. Nothing exists on CMB. The decisive finding: in chest X-ray, linear probes on the CLS token of frozen RAD-DINO, DINOv2, DINOv3, BiomedCLIP and MedSigLIP stayed at chance for 4-8 px perturbations, while pooling the patch tokens inside the lesion box from the same forward pass reached AUC >= 0.899 on natural small lesions, +0.119 to +0.387 over CLS (preprint; needs the lesion location, which our stage 1 provides) [11]. In a CT detector with 82 % of lesions < 6 mm a frozen natural-image DINOv2 with registers beat the medical encoders (CPM 0.518 vs 0.423-0.433, preprint) [12]. Against: 3D CT foundation models stay near chance on small nodules [13]; on renal lesions no foundation model beat a ResNet-50 from scratch and radiomics did best (external AUC 0.88 vs 0.69-0.77) [14]; upsampling small native crops costs accuracy (MedMNIST 28 px -> 224: AUC 91.75 vs 96.62 at native 224) [15]. Expected: parity with the stage-2 CNN, at best a complement in a late-fusion average. Main risks: natural-image encoders may not separate bleeds from vessel cross-sections on magnitude images, and features may encode site rather than lesion.

Trägt

Three new stage-2 arms for Arena 2, all on the frozen out-of-fold candidate pool and the frozen folds, judged by the v2 decision rules: - T (tabular): LightGBM, shallow trees, strong regularisation, nested scan-grouped CV, features above; plus T+E (same model with the CNN embedding appended). CPU, seconds per fit. Run this FIRST: it is the baseline every TrackSet module must beat, and it tells whether the slice-track idea is already captured by a hand-made tube score. - F (frozen): DINOv2-B/14 with registers (second arm MedSigLIP-448), 16 mm in-plane crop, channels = slices -1/0/+1 (mIP over +-2 slices for thin SWI), bicubic upsampling to 224, mean of the central 2x2-3x3 patch tokens from the last and one middle block, L2 logistic regression with nested C; never the CLS token alone. - U (unlabelled): one noisy-student round on stage 2 only: stage 1 frozen on unlabelled scans of the same sequences, current stage 2 as teacher, keep only p > 0.95 positives and p < 0.05 negatives, student with stronger augmentation, 5 paired seeds. Unlabelled in-house scans are class 3 data: local profile only.

Trägt nicht

Any expectation of a large standalone gain from F or U; the effect-size ranges are the agents' extrapolations, not measurements. No source measured any of the three levers on CMB candidates. Most sources were read at abstract level; FocalMix and Orlando tables were read in full text; several 2026 sources are preprints.

Bruchindikator

T falls clearly below the stage-2 CNN on all cohorts (then the few-positives argument does not hold here); F with patch-local pooling stays near chance on our candidates; U produces more confident positives on veins than on bleeds.
