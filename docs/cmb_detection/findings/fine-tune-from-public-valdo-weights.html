Fine-tuning from the public VALDO weights beats training from scratch; unadapted they over-detect

Aussage

The anisotropic U-Net trained on all 72 public VALDO cases (released weights, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): 1. Unadapted on another T2* cohort it over-detects at the released stage-1 cell (threshold 0.6, 2 mm3): lesion F1 rose monotonically with the threshold up to 0.9 (+0.09 F1 from 0.6 to 0.9, the best cell chosen on the target cases, so optimistic); it stays clearly below a model trained in the target domain. Full-data models give higher probabilities than cross-validation models (also noted in the release documentation). 2. Fine-tuned (all weights initialised from VALDO, train.lr 1e-4, 6 epochs, same recipe otherwise, ~7 min on one GPU): +0.11 F1 against 6 epochs from scratch and +0.03 against the 60-epoch from-scratch training on the same fold. With the default learning rate 3e-4 the gain was smaller (+0.04): a large learning rate destroys part of the start. 3. Channel changes are possible: input channels are matched by name; a new channel starts at zero weight, so the network first reproduces the pretrained output exactly (unit test) and learns the new channel during training.

Trägt

Weights load bit-identically in the training package and in the release package (max |difference| 0.0). Fine-tuning gain 0.11 is above the seed noise of this recipe (0.03-0.08).

Trägt nicht

One fold, one seed: the +0.03 against 60 epochs from scratch is inside the noise; needs 5 folds x 2 seeds before it becomes a rule. Numbers of the in-house cohort are kept private (relative differences only here).

Bruchindikator

A 5-fold, 2-seed repetition where fine-tuning (lr 1e-4) does not beat short scratch training by more than the noise.

Ablesung

VALDO: Sudre et al., Med Image Anal 2024;91:103029, doi:10.1016/j.media.2023.103029, PMID 37988921. Released weights: https://github.com/mendeltem/microbleed_detection_3D_UNET/releases/tag/v0.1.0 (SHA-256 checked). Transfer learning in medical imaging, background: Raghu et al., NeurIPS 2019, "Transfusion: Understanding Transfer Learning for Medical Imaging" (arXiv:1902.07208). Own runs 2026-10-16 (cmbx, fold 0, seed 42).
