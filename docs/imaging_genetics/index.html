Aussage

Mouse data. I found no public dataset with a per-animal genome sequence plus brain MRI. The genetics in mouse MRI collections is a strain or a model label. A label still links to DNA, because the strains themselves are sequenced: the Mouse Genomes Project sequenced 17 inbred strains, including C57BL/6J, DBA/2J and CAST/EiJ (Keane 2011), and the Mouse Phenome Database serves strain-level variant calls. So "strain X + MRI of strain X + genome of strain X" can be assembled. The number of distinct genotypes stays small (4 to about 26 per collection).

Human counterparts (genome + brain MRI): UK Biobank (application and fee; new applications reported paused, not confirmed), ADNI (whole-genome sequencing under a separate agreement). Neither offers radiology text as a ready-made training corpus (not checked in detail).

Models. - DNA language models: Nucleotide Transformer (50 M-2.5 B parameters, Dalla-Torre 2024), HyenaDNA (up to 1 M tokens), Caduceus, DNABERT-2, Evo 2 (1-40 B parameters, up to 1 M context, weights on Hugging Face). - MRI and MRI+text models: BrainIAC (brain-MRI foundation model, about 49,000 scans, Tak 2026), RadFM and M3D (3D medical images + text). - DNA + text: ChatNT, and BioReason (Nucleotide Transformer or Evo 2 encoder feeding a Qwen3 language model; checkpoints announced, not released). - Genotype + MRI, contrastive shared embedding: AlzCLIP (SNPs + MRI features, ADNI 758 + UK Biobank 261 subjects) and COMICAL (genetic markers + imaging-derived phenotypes, UK Biobank; code and weights public). - No published model combines all three modalities.

Assessment (mine, not from the sources): how a "multilingual" DNA + MRI + English model would be built. - Architecture: the LLaVA pattern. Frozen modality encoders feed small projection layers into an LLM, and only the projectors plus LoRA adapters are trained. The encoders would be a DNA model (e.g. Evo 2 1B or Nucleotide Transformer 500M) and a 3D brain-MRI encoder (e.g. BrainIAC). BioReason already does the DNA half this way. - The bottleneck is paired data. Training needs examples that have all three parts at once: DNA, MRI, and a text description. - In mouse, the text can be generated from metadata ("5XFAD, female, 6 months, smaller hippocampus"). DNA then means the transgene or the strain's variant set. - Mouse collections offer only tens of distinct genotypes, so a model cannot learn a general DNA -> brain mapping. It can learn a narrow task such as "genotype -> regional-volume signature -> description". - A cheaper first step: a contrastive genotype-MRI embedding (as in COMICAL or AlzCLIP) plus a language model that explains it via tool calls, instead of one end-to-end model. - Hardware: a 24 GB card can hold the frozen encoders, a 1-4 B LLM and LoRA training on small data. - Patient data: genome + MRI + reports of patients must stay on the local machine; a cloud model never sees them.

Nachtrag 2026-10-24: the clinical layer (cognitive scores, age, diagnosis)

A DNA + MRI + text model for dementia also needs the structured clinical layer, measured in the same people and dated, ideally at several visits.

What the layer contains. - Demographics: age at each visit, sex, years of education, ancestry. - Cognitive screening and staging: MoCA with version and language, MMSE, CDR. - Neuropsychological battery: for example the NACC UDS 3.0, which comes with norms by age, sex and education (Weintraub 2018). - Diagnosis: clinical diagnosis and aetiology. - Fluid and PET biomarkers: amyloid and tau PET, CSF, plasma p-tau, NfL, GFAP. - Genetics: APOE genotype. - Health: vascular and lifestyle risk factors, as in the Lancet Commission 2024 report; medication and stroke history. - MRI context: derived MRI measures (hippocampal volume, WMH, microbleeds, infarcts) and acquisition metadata (scanner, field strength, sequence).

Why each part matters. - Scores are not interchangeable. The MoCA catches MCI that the MMSE misses: in the original validation the MoCA had 90 % sensitivity for MCI against 18 % for the MMSE (Nasreddine 2005). MoCA scores run consistently lower than MMSE scores. Equipercentile conversion tables exist: - a German-speaking memory clinic, n = 803, plus a review of 19 conversion studies (Fasnacht 2022); - by dementia type (Roheger 2022); - in Parkinson's disease (van Steenoven 2014), validated across 1,161 patients with a mean difference of 0.05 points, SD 1.84 (Lawton 2016). - Raw scores need norms. Age, sex and education shift both test scores and brain volumes, so the model should see z-scores against norms, not only raw values. - Change over time is the strongest signal. In ADNI's first year, 16.5 % of MCI participants progressed to dementia (Petersen 2010).

How a language model takes it in (assessment). - Serialize the values as text with a fixed schema, units and dates. Write "missing" explicitly. Example: "age 72, female, education 12 y, MoCA 23/30 (v8.1, German), CDR-SB 1.5, APOE e3/e4". - An LLM already knows what MoCA or CDR mean. What it lacks is the pairing with images and genomes in the same people. - A separate tabular encoder is the alternative when exact arithmetic on many variables matters.

Datasets with the clinical layer. - Human: ADNI (cognitive and functional measures, CSF, MRI/PET, longitudinal; Petersen 2010) and NACC (UDS battery; Weintraub 2018). - Mouse: no cognitive scores; behavioural tests are the analogue. MODEL-AD includes behaviour (Oblak 2021).

Nachtrag 2026-10-24: the time axis ("4D": 3D MRI over years)

Understanding a disease like AD means knowing its course, not one snapshot. Four model families exist.

Event order. - The event-based model learns the order of events across a cohort, without predefined clinical stages, from cross-sectional or longitudinal data (Fonteijn 2012, familial AD and Huntington's disease). - SuStaIn adds subtypes, each with its own trajectory (Young 2018). It finds three AD subtypes, identifies genetic FTD genotypes from imaging alone, and predicts diagnostic conversion better than models without subtype or stage.

Individual image forecasting. Brain Latent Progression predicts a person's future 3D T1 MRI with a latent diffusion model conditioned on subject metadata, with voxel-level uncertainty (Puglisi 2025). It was trained on 11,730 MRIs from 2,805 subjects and tested externally on 2,257 MRIs from 962 subjects; the code is public.

Digital twin. Operator learning of the spatiotemporal spread of amyloid, tau and neurodegeneration per patient, used to simulate interventions in silico (Wang 2026; the authors claim >90 % prediction accuracy).

Assessment (mine). - The building blocks for a "brain world model" exist separately: - an MRI encoder gives a latent brain state; - a dynamics module (BrLP-like) moves that state in time, conditioned on age, genotype (APOE or a DNA embedding) and clinical scores; - an LLM on top reads and explains the result. - The data must be repeated visits of the same people with dates, scores and comparable scanners. - "What if" simulations are only as good as the causal knowledge behind them. Associations such as neuroticism -> dementia (see dementia_risk_factors) must not be fed in as causes.

Trägt

Choosing data and an architecture for a first genotype-MRI-text prototype; knowing that mouse MRI data carry labels, and that strain genomes have to be joined in separately.

Trägt nicht

Claims about model quality (none of the multimodal models was run here); open-weight status of Nucleotide Transformer, HyenaDNA, Caduceus, DNABERT-2, RadFM, M3D and BrainIAC (not checked); exact access terms of UK Biobank and ADNI today.

Bruchindikator

A public mouse dataset with per-animal sequencing and brain MRI (e.g. Diversity Outbred mice with MRI), or a published model that takes DNA, 3D MRI and text together.
