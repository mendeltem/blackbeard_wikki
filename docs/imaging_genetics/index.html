Kurzstand

Stand 2026-10-24. Prefill für Chats und Agenten. Details in INDEX.md.

2026-10-24, scan: one native multimodal model instead of cooperating models. The documented recipe is a cheap tokenizer per modality, one transformer with shared attention, and modality-specific feed-forward weights or experts against interference (Mixture-of-Transformers, MoMa). Whether modalities compete depends on model size relative to data and on the modality pair: speech-text needed 30 B to turn competition into synergy, but translation models under 1 B largely avoid interference, and early fusion is not worse than late fusion at small size (counter-checked 2026-10-24). Science models unify sequences (NatureLM, Evo 2, ESM3, Galactica); generalists are usually competitive but lose on precision tasks. No verified model takes text, DNA, 3D MRI and clinical numbers natively. See one-native-multimodal-model-vs-model-ensembles-2026-10.

2026-10-24, scan: public mouse brain MRI comes with genotype labels (strain, knockout, transgene), not per-animal genomes; strain genomes (Mouse Genomes Project, Mouse Phenome Database) can be joined by label. Openly downloadable: Cambridge Huntington's library (399 brains) and MODEL-AD data via the AD Knowledge Portal. Models exist for DNA (Nucleotide Transformer, Evo 2, HyenaDNA), brain MRI (BrainIAC), MRI + text (RadFM, M3D), DNA + text (ChatNT, BioReason) and SNP + MRI (AlzCLIP, COMICAL); none takes all three. See mouse-genotype-mri-data-and-dna-mri-text-models-2026-10.

Aussage

Mouse data. I found no public dataset with a per-animal genome sequence plus brain MRI. The genetics in mouse MRI collections is a strain or a model label. A label still links to DNA, because the strains themselves are sequenced: the Mouse Genomes Project sequenced 17 inbred strains, including C57BL/6J, DBA/2J and CAST/EiJ (Keane 2011), and the Mouse Phenome Database serves strain-level variant calls. So "strain X + MRI of strain X + genome of strain X" can be assembled. The number of distinct genotypes stays small (4 to about 26 per collection).

Human counterparts (genome + brain MRI): UK Biobank (application and fee; new applications reported paused, not confirmed), ADNI (whole-genome sequencing under a separate agreement). Neither offers radiology text as a ready-made training corpus (not checked in detail).

Models. - DNA language models: Nucleotide Transformer (50 M-2.5 B parameters, Dalla-Torre 2024), HyenaDNA (up to 1 M tokens), Caduceus, DNABERT-2, Evo 2 (1-40 B parameters, up to 1 M context, weights on Hugging Face). - MRI and MRI+text models: BrainIAC (brain-MRI foundation model, about 49,000 scans, Tak 2026), RadFM and M3D (3D medical images + text). - DNA + text: ChatNT, and BioReason (Nucleotide Transformer or Evo 2 encoder feeding a Qwen3 language model; checkpoints announced, not released). - Genotype + MRI, contrastive shared embedding: AlzCLIP (SNPs + MRI features, ADNI 758 + UK Biobank 261 subjects) and COMICAL (genetic markers + imaging-derived phenotypes, UK Biobank; code and weights public). - No published model combines all three modalities.

Assessment (mine, not from the sources): how a "multilingual" DNA + MRI + English model would be built. - Architecture: the LLaVA pattern. Frozen modality encoders feed small projection layers into an LLM, and only the projectors plus LoRA adapters are trained. The encoders would be a DNA model (e.g. Evo 2 1B or Nucleotide Transformer 500M) and a 3D brain-MRI encoder (e.g. BrainIAC). BioReason already does the DNA half this way. - The bottleneck is paired data. Training needs examples that have all three parts at once: DNA, MRI, and a text description. - In mouse, the text can be generated from metadata ("5XFAD, female, 6 months, smaller hippocampus"). DNA then means the transgene or the strain's variant set. - Mouse collections offer only tens of distinct genotypes, so a model cannot learn a general DNA -> brain mapping. It can learn a narrow task such as "genotype -> regional-volume signature -> description". - A cheaper first step: a contrastive genotype-MRI embedding (as in COMICAL or AlzCLIP) plus a language model that explains it via tool calls, instead of one end-to-end model. - Hardware: a 24 GB card can hold the frozen encoders, a 1-4 B LLM and LoRA training on small data. - Patient data: genome + MRI + reports of patients must stay on the local machine; a cloud model never sees them.

Nachtrag 2026-10-24: the clinical layer (cognitive scores, age, diagnosis)

A DNA + MRI + text model for dementia also needs the structured clinical layer, measured in the same people and dated, ideally at several visits.

What the layer contains. - Demographics: age at each visit, sex, years of education, ancestry. - Cognitive screening and staging: MoCA with version and language, MMSE, CDR. - Neuropsychological battery: for example the NACC UDS 3.0, which comes with norms by age, sex and education (Weintraub 2018). - Diagnosis: clinical diagnosis and aetiology. - Fluid and PET biomarkers: amyloid and tau PET, CSF, plasma p-tau, NfL, GFAP. - Genetics: APOE genotype. - Health: vascular and lifestyle risk factors, as in the Lancet Commission 2024 report; medication and stroke history. - MRI context: derived MRI measures (hippocampal volume, WMH, microbleeds, infarcts) and acquisition metadata (scanner, field strength, sequence).

Why each part matters. - Scores are not interchangeable. The MoCA catches MCI that the MMSE misses: in the original validation the MoCA had 90 % sensitivity for MCI against 18 % for the MMSE (Nasreddine 2005). MoCA scores run consistently lower than MMSE scores. Equipercentile conversion tables exist: - a German-speaking memory clinic, n = 803, plus a review of 19 conversion studies (Fasnacht 2022); - by dementia type (Roheger 2022); - in Parkinson's disease (van Steenoven 2014), validated across 1,161 patients with a mean difference of 0.05 points, SD 1.84 (Lawton 2016). - Raw scores need norms. Age, sex and education shift both test scores and brain volumes, so the model should see z-scores against norms, not only raw values. - Change over time is the strongest signal. In ADNI's first year, 16.5 % of MCI participants progressed to dementia (Petersen 2010).

How a language model takes it in (assessment). - Serialize the values as text with a fixed schema, units and dates. Write "missing" explicitly. Example: "age 72, female, education 12 y, MoCA 23/30 (v8.1, German), CDR-SB 1.5, APOE e3/e4". - An LLM already knows what MoCA or CDR mean. What it lacks is the pairing with images and genomes in the same people. - A separate tabular encoder is the alternative when exact arithmetic on many variables matters.

Datasets with the clinical layer. - Human: ADNI (cognitive and functional measures, CSF, MRI/PET, longitudinal; Petersen 2010) and NACC (UDS battery; Weintraub 2018). - Mouse: no cognitive scores; behavioural tests are the analogue. MODEL-AD includes behaviour (Oblak 2021).

Nachtrag 2026-10-24: the time axis ("4D": 3D MRI over years)

Understanding a disease like AD means knowing its course, not one snapshot. Four model families exist.

Event order. - The event-based model learns the order of events across a cohort, without predefined clinical stages, from cross-sectional or longitudinal data (Fonteijn 2012, familial AD and Huntington's disease). - SuStaIn adds subtypes, each with its own trajectory (Young 2018). It finds three AD subtypes, identifies genetic FTD genotypes from imaging alone, and predicts diagnostic conversion better than models without subtype or stage.

Individual image forecasting. Brain Latent Progression predicts a person's future 3D T1 MRI with a latent diffusion model conditioned on subject metadata, with voxel-level uncertainty (Puglisi 2025). It was trained on 11,730 MRIs from 2,805 subjects and tested externally on 2,257 MRIs from 962 subjects; the code is public.

Digital twin. Operator learning of the spatiotemporal spread of amyloid, tau and neurodegeneration per patient, used to simulate interventions in silico (Wang 2026; the authors claim >90 % prediction accuracy).

Assessment (mine). - The building blocks for a "brain world model" exist separately: - an MRI encoder gives a latent brain state; - a dynamics module (BrLP-like) moves that state in time, conditioned on age, genotype (APOE or a DNA embedding) and clinical scores; - an LLM on top reads and explains the result. - The data must be repeated visits of the same people with dates, scores and comparable scanners. - "What if" simulations are only as good as the causal knowledge behind them. Associations such as neuroticism -> dementia (see dementia_risk_factors) must not be fed in as causes.

Nachtrag 2026-10-24: the other "languages" - chemistry, physics, biology across scales

Each level of biology has its own data form, which works like a language, and each already has a model family. The levels are linked physically. For AD the chain would run: APOE (DNA) -> protein folding and clearance -> microglial gene expression -> tau spread along the connectome -> atrophy on MRI -> MoCA decline -> daily life and care.

Assessment (mine). - No model learns physics or chemistry from text alone. There are two routes: - learned surrogate models per level, which are fast but approximate; - an LLM as conductor that calls exact simulators and specialist models as tools (AlphaFold, molecular dynamics, The Virtual Brain, network diffusion), with the LLM supplying the textbook-level glue between levels. - The realistic system is a hybrid. - The hard gap is crossing scales, from nanoseconds and nanometres to decades and centimetres. No dataset pairs molecular data with MRI and behaviour in the same person, so the levels have to be bridged by intermediate models. The network diffusion model is one such bridge, from protein spread to an MRI pattern.

Aussage

The recipe. - Every modality gets its own cheap tokenizer or patch layer: text BPE, image VQ codes or continuous patches, DNA nucleotides, protein residues, SMILES, numbers. - One transformer with shared attention processes the mixed sequence. - Each modality gets its own output head or loss.

Five design families are documented: 1. Discrete early fusion, everything as tokens in one decoder: Chameleon, Emu3, Gato, Unified-IO 2. 2. Hybrid losses in one backbone: Transfusion uses next-token loss for text and diffusion for images, scales better than quantizing images (models up to 7 B), and Show-o is similar. 3. Decoupled visual front ends around one backbone: Janus. 4. Modality-specific parameters inside one model: - Mixture-of-Transformers matches the dense baseline with 55.8 % of the FLOPs. - MoMa (modality-aware experts) saves 3.7x FLOPs. 5. Shared perception encoders or embedding spaces: Perceiver IO, ImageBind, Meta-Transformer, 4M/4M-21 (any-to-any over tens of modalities).

Interference is real, and it depends on scale. - Mixed-modal scaling laws model synergy and competition between modalities explicitly. A 30 B speech-text model beat the unimodal models (Aghajanyan 2023). In the paper body only the 30 B model is reported as crossing the "competition barrier" (350 M and 2.7 B were also trained; checked). - A study of 457 natively multimodal models found no inherent advantage of late fusion (bolt-on encoders) over early fusion; mixture-of-experts lets the model learn modality-specific weights (Shukor 2025). - Chameleon needed extra stabilization because shared weights let the modalities compete: each modality "tries to compete" by slowly growing its norms until bf16 training diverges. The 7 B model needed QK-norm, dropout and z-loss, the 34 B model z-loss only (paper body, checked by the main session). - Best-supported fix: keep attention shared and give each modality its own feed-forward weights or experts.

Scientific "languages of nature" in one model. - Sequence-only unification works: - NatureLM: molecules, proteins, DNA/RNA and materials as tagged sequences in one model. - Galactica: SMILES and protein sequences inline in scientific text. - Intern-S1: a dynamic tokenizer for SMILES/FASTA, plus a time-series encoder. - Evo 2: DNA, with RNA and protein reached through the central dogma. - ESM3: protein sequence, structure and function tokens in one model. - Generalist biomedical models: Med-PaLM M (text, images, genomics; 14 tasks), BiomedGPT, MedGemma, RadFM. - Generalists are usually competitive but lose on precision tasks. - TxGemma was compared against specialists on 66 tasks: superior on 26, superior or comparable on 50. - Tx-LLM exceeds state of the art on 22 of 66 tasks. - Med-PaLM M loses to the dedicated DeepVariant on variant calling: Indel-F1 97.04 % vs 99.40 %. DeepVariant had 2,633 times more training examples; with the same examples the gap narrows to 97.04 % vs 98.55 %, and for SNPs Med-PaLM M beats GATK4 (99.35 % vs 99.29 %) (paper body, checked). - 3D MRI in a text model is expensive. MedGemma 1.5 feeds up to 85 axial slices, 21,760 vision tokens in total. It is not truly 3D: its image encoder takes only 2D RGB images trained with 256 intensity values per channel (paper body, checked). RadFM compresses 3D patches to 32 tokens per image with a Perceiver. - Numbers. Galactica splits digits, Tx-LLM and TxGemma bin regression targets, and xVal proposes one continuous number token. - No verified model covers text + DNA/RNA + protein + molecules + 3D MRI + clinical numbers natively.

Assessment (mine): a practical path to one model. 1. Start from an open LLM backbone. 2. Add modality tokenizers: nucleotides for DNA, 3D patch tokens compressed to a few dozen per scan for MRI, and numbers as text with units or an xVal-style continuous token. 3. Give DNA, MRI and tabular tokens their own feed-forward weights (Mixture-of-Transformers style) behind shared attention. 4. Train in two stages: align first with frozen encoders (cheap), then train jointly on mixed paired and unpaired data.

Native pretraining from scratch is data-centre scale. On a single 24 GB card the realistic options are continued training or LoRA of an existing multimodal model at a few billion parameters. Corrected after the counter-check below: whether modalities compete at that size depends on the model size relative to the data and on the modality pair. It is not fixed by parameter count alone.

Gegenprüfung 2026-10-24 (adversarial pass plus full-text checks)

C1, shared attention plus modality-specific weights is the best fix: holds with limits. - Mixture-of-Transformers is supported, and BAGEL (1.5 B) found MoT better than MoE better than dense at equal FLOPs. - In sparse models, learned modality-agnostic routing beat hand-assigned modality-aware routing (Shukor 2025, paper body). - Cheaper fixes target the same interference: gradient modulation, balancing learning speed, data sampling ratios. - The multilingual analogue: language-specific modules lift the "curse of multilinguality" (Pfeiffer 2022).

C2, competition at small scale: holds with limits, partly contradicted. - What holds: Aghajanyan measured 350 M, 2.7 B and 30 B speech-text models. Only the 30 B model crossed the "competition barrier"; the predicted optimum was N = 28.35 B, D = 45.12 B tokens. - In translation, substantial interference occurs mainly when the model is very small relative to its data; standard transformers below one billion parameters largely avoid it, and the sampling temperature controls the balance (Shaham 2023). - Early fusion shows a slight advantage over late fusion at small model sizes (Shukor 2025). - Multilingual LMs up to 45 M parameters gain from moderate added multilingual data and lose from heavy amounts (Chang 2023). - The two kinds of comparison answer different questions. Joint vs separate unimodal models shows competition at small scale. Early vs late fusion inside one multimodal model shows early fusion is not worse.

C3, generalists lose on precision; specialist teams can win: holds with limits. - Specialists win on precision: DeepVariant beats Med-PaLM M (above), and fine-tuned BERT/BART beat zero- or few-shot LLMs on most BioNLP tasks. - Tools help: GeneGPT with NCBI web APIs scores 0.83 vs 0.44 for Bing, and an open-model multi-agent system matches or beats GeneGPT on over 90 % of tasks. - But a prompted generalist (GPT-4 with Medprompt) beat the specialist-tuned Med-PaLM 2 on all nine MultiMedQA sets. - Small LLMs are weak tool callers (Shen 2024), so tool teams at small scale need the tool use split across models.

C4, numbers and 3D volumes are weak points: holds for current designs, not intrinsic. - Eight 3D medical VLMs averaged 34 % accuracy on semantic-spatial CT questions, often below random. - Arithmetic depends on tokenization; program-aided LMs offload the arithmetic to code. - A better 3D tokenizer raised clinical F1 by 40 % (Hamamci 2025), and LLMs forecast numeric series zero-shot.

Net change: the design recipe stands. The claim that small models necessarily see more competition than synergy is withdrawn; it depends on model size relative to data, on the modality pair and on the data mix.

Trägt

Architecture choice for a multi-"language" model: shared attention plus modality-specific weights; which tokenization exists per modality; what the generalist-vs-specialist evidence says.

Trägt nicht

Performance claims for DNA + 3D MRI + text in one model (none published and verified); training budgets of the cited models (not extracted); weight licences beyond what the abstracts state.

Bruchindikator

A published natively multimodal model that includes 3D MRI and genomic sequence and beats specialist ensembles; or scaling evidence that small mixed models (< 10 B) already gain from adding modalities.
