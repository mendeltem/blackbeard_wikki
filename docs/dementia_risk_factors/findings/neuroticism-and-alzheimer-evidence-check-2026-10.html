Neuroticism and Alzheimer's disease: a consistent but modest risk association; "more plaques at autopsy" and "causal genetics" do not hold

Aussage

The association is real and replicated, but smaller than "twice the risk". Higher neuroticism (or "distress proneness", a short neuroticism scale) predicts incident Alzheimer's disease (AD) and all-cause dementia in many prospective cohorts since 2003. Pooled estimates are modest: HR 1.13 per scale unit (Low 2013, N = 3,285), HR 1.24 (Aschwanden 2021, N about 30,000-33,000), AD HR 1.07 (Franks 2021, 497 cases). Large single cohorts: UK Biobank top vs bottom quartile AD HR 1.42 and all-cause 1.70 (Terracciano 2021, N = 401,422); EPIC-Norfolk HR 1.14 per SD over a median 22.7 years (Gao 2025). The "more than twice the risk" comes from the early Rush studies, which compare the 90th with the 10th percentile and had 55-140 incident cases.

Autopsy: neuroticism goes with less resilience, not with more pathology. The Rush clinical-pathologic studies found distress proneness unrelated to plaques, tangles, Lewy bodies and infarcts (Wilson 2006, Wilson 2007), and the individual-participant meta-analysis of eight studies found "no consistent associations with neuropathology" (Beck, autopsy N = 1,703). What autopsy work does show: at equal AD pathology, people high in neuroticism are less likely to stay free of dementia (Terracciano 2013, n = 111, OR 2.0; Graham 2021, n = 1,375). The one "more pathology" signal is a correlation of r = 0.26 with tangle stage in the n = 111 sample.

PET: small pooled effects, many null studies. Terracciano 2022 (BLSA, cognitively normal) found neuroticism with higher cortical amyloid (OR 1.68), and pooled 12 amyloid studies (r = 0.07, N = 3,015) and 8 tau studies (r = 0.15, N = 2,231). Several PET cohorts found no neuroticism-amyloid association (Byun 2020, n = 397; Yoon 2020; Oh 2022; Giannakopoulos 2022). Positive tau-PET findings in medial temporal regions come from small samples (Schultz 2020, n = 128; Parent 2022, n = 47). CSF and autosomal-dominant AD studies suggest the reverse arrow as well: AD pathology raising neuroticism (Tautvydaite 2017; Aschenbrenner 2020).

Genetics: no causal evidence for neuroticism -> AD. - Neuroticism is highly polygenic: 136 loci (Nagel 2018), 116 loci (Luciano 2018). - Cortical structure is genetically correlated with neuroticism (Grasby 2020). That is a shared genetic signal, not proof of causation. - The one Mendelian randomization (MR) study with AD as the outcome is null (Ma 2021, beta -0.0098, p = 0.262). Its authors attribute the observational link to residual confounding. - MR of neuroticism on brain structure supports only white-matter diffusivity measures (Gao 2025, Psychological Medicine; Zhao 2024), not cortical thickness. - The Molecular Psychiatry polygenic-score paper (Opel 2018) found lower surface area and states that no associations with cortical thickness were detected. - In UK Biobank, a higher neuroticism polygenic score went with less cognitive decline (Akbarian 2024, n = 645 at follow-up). Conversely, AD genetic risk raises neuroticism (Stephan 2018).

Reverse causation is partly but not fully excluded. BLSA found no preclinical change in neuroticism before dementia (Terracciano 2017), while OCTO-Twin, LASA and the Einstein Aging Study found rising neuroticism before diagnosis (Yoneda 2017, 2020). A 2025 meta-analysis puts self-reported preclinical change at SMD 0-0.2. The association persists after 20 or more years of follow-up (EPIC-Norfolk HR 1.09), which argues against a purely prodromal effect.

The plausible pathway is vascular and mental health. - In UK Biobank and EPIC-Norfolk the association is partly mediated by depression, anxiety, hypertension and ischaemic heart disease, and it is stronger for vascular dementia (Gao 2024: +11 % all-cause, +15 % vascular). - Neuroticism goes with more white matter hyperintensities; about 40 % of that is explained by vascular risk factors (Terracciano 2023, n = 40,602). - Long-standing distress mediates the midlife-neuroticism link in a 38-year cohort of women (Johansson 2014). - Cortisol/HPA evidence is weak: small samples, and a cortisol effect in men only (Montoliu 2021).

Prevention is untested. Neuroticism falls with therapy (meta-analysis d about 0.37 over about 24 weeks; Roberts 2017). No trial has a dementia outcome, and 18 months of meditation in older adults did not change the volume of the target brain regions (Age-Well; Chételat 2022).

Trägt

Risk communication and study design: neuroticism is a replicated, modest, partly mediated risk marker for dementia, somewhat stronger for vascular than for Alzheimer dementia. A study with personality or distress questionnaires plus structural MRI can test the vascular path directly (WMH, grey-matter volume) instead of assuming an amyloid path.

Trägt nicht

Claims that neuroticism causes AD, raises plaque or tangle load at autopsy, or that lowering it prevents dementia. Individual risk prediction: the per-SD effect (HR about 1.1-1.2) is small.

Bruchindikator

  • An MR study with current neuroticism instruments (Nagel 2018 or larger) and the newest AD GWAS shows a robust non-null effect with pleiotropy checks.
  • A large autopsy or longitudinal PET study shows that neuroticism predicts accumulation of amyloid or tau.
  • A trial that lowers neuroticism or distress changes dementia incidence or biomarkers.

Ablesung

How the check was done. - Searched PubMed on 2026-10-24 in four passes (three narrow sub-passes plus one by the main session). - Every PMID below was fetched again with PubMed get_article_metadata. First author, year, journal and DOI match. The key numbers were checked against the fetched abstracts. - Not checked against full texts. One sub-pass read the BLSA PET full text (PMC). It reported that personality was a single measure taken mostly within a year of the scan. That point was not re-checked here. - Bias note: genetic liability to neuroticism and to AD lowers participation in the optional UK Biobank components such as imaging (Tyrrell 2021). Neuroticism-MRI results from UK Biobank can therefore be distorted.

Not covered. - No Mendelian randomization of AD -> neuroticism was found. - No neuroticism x AD genetic-correlation value appears in any fetched abstract. - Not searched: Chinese cohorts, HRS and MIDUS beyond the papers listed, and preprints.
