Software block mandatory (English, for external readers)

Every quality-control page of every viewer must carry a mandatory "Software" block, in English, listing every program and version that produced the numbers and images on that page: the MRI package with its build stamp and the exact segmentation flags, the atlas/registration tools, all deep-learning models with their weights file and training set, the DICOM converter version as recorded in the BIDS sidecars, the Python interpreter and package versions, the operating system and host, and the build date and time. Versions are collected automatically at build time from the tools themselves (build stamps, version files, git commits, sidecar JSON); anything a tool cannot report is printed as "not recorded ", never omitted. The block appears as a "Software" section in the page (Methods overlay or dedicated section) and as a one-line footer "Built with FreeSurfer x.y, SynthSeg (flags), FSL x.y, ...". It contains no patient data. DEMDAS implements this in qc_software.py (demdas-gm), shared by gm_qc_axial.py, verlauf_statistik.py, fehler_statistik.py and abc_qc_bauen.py, plus an English "Software" sheet in DEMDAS_GM_Beispiel.xlsx.

Aussage

Die CMB-Qualitaetskontrolle ist die Vorlage. Fuer jede neue Laesionsart (PVS, WMH, Infarkt, Lakunen) gilt dieselbe Reihenfolge; nur die Lader und die Wortwahl wechseln.

  1. Erst der Generator, nie eine neue Seite. Die Seite (PAGE) bleibt, es kommt ein Lader je Datenlage dazu: Manifest mit id und Pfaden, Bild, Referenz oder Vergleichsmaske, je Lauf eine Wahrscheinlichkeits- oder Maskendatei mit Schwelle. Gemessen: der PVS-Betrachter fuer 73 Faelle war mit einem Lader von 150 Zeilen in einer Stunde fertig, weil PAGE, Umriss, Farben und Tasten aus dem CMB-Generator kamen.
  2. Ohne Handreferenz: die Vergleichsmaske heisst Vergleich. Links blau steht dann die Pipeline- oder Fremdmaske, auf der Seite ausdruecklich "not a reference"; alle Zaehler heissen Uebereinstimmung ("both / only method / only pipeline"), nie TP/FP/FN. Die gepoolten Zahlen der Seite muessen den Zahlen des Messskripts gleichen (BELOVE: behalten 9.591 / 2.120 / 33.237 = F6.2), sonst ist ein Lader falsch.
  3. Ein Modell: Urteilsfarben. Mehrere Modelle: Modellfarben. Gruen erkannt, gelb Fehlalarm, rot verpasst (--verdict); beim Vergleich mehrerer Laeufe die Goldener-Schnitt-Farben ohne das blaue Band.
  4. Jede Regel bekommt eigene Ebenen, bevor sie in die Kette kommt. Die Karte, auf der die Regel beruht (SynthSeg-Liquor als Fuellung), die Komponenten, die sie als Fehlalarm entfernt, und die, die sie entfernt, obwohl sie eine Referenz beruehren; Zaehler je Schicht, je Fall, gepoolt. Die Liquor-Regel loeschte so sichtbar 62 von 426 Treffern auf SWI, was die Summenzahl verdeckt hatte.
  5. Jeder Fund braucht sein Warum. Je Komponente: Groesse in Voxeln und mm3, Zahl der Schichten, Form, Lage nach Gewebekarte, p max, Zuordnung zur Referenz, und die Nachbarschichten als Stapel. Ein Gefaess laeuft durch die Schichten, eine Mikroblutung nicht; das sieht man nur im Stapel, nicht in der Schicht.
  6. Rating ist eine eigene Seite, nicht die QC-Seite. Verblindet (p und Pipeline-Treffer erst nach dem Urteil), Reihenfolge zufaellig mit festem Seed je Fall, jedes Urteil sofort per POST in eine Datei je Fall, Definition der Laesion (STRIVE-2) in der LIESMICH, Zusatzfunde per Klick fuer den Recall der Decke, Auswerteskript liefert nur Summen je Verfahren und Groessenklasse.
  7. Patientendaten nur auf 127.0.0.1. Live-Server im Projektordner, kein LAN, kein GitHub, keine Fallnummern auf der Tafel oder im Wiki. Oeffentlich nur oeffentliche Daten mit Lizenzzeile (VALDO: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).
  8. Pruefung vor der Abgabe: headless rendern bei 1400 und 1918 px, Zaehler gegen das Messskript, Rauchtest (Dateien laden, Konsole leer, bei Rating zwei Urteile schreiben und lesen), Rauchtest-Urteile danach beiseitelegen.
  9. Vorlesung ist ein eigenes Artefakt. Animation oder Deck zeigen die Kette mit echten Schichten und echten Funden, aber sie ersetzen die QC-Seite nicht und kommen nicht in den QC-Ordner.
  10. Nach jeder Seite drei Zeilen hier: was neu war, welche Setzung der Nutzer geaendert hat, welche Zahl die Seite bestaetigt oder widerlegt hat.

BefundLiterature: can vision-language models pre-sort medical image / segmentation QC?vorschlag2026-10-18Literature (30 refs, checked): no study shows autonomous VLM QC of segmentation overlays; orientation and image-only questions are weak, over-calling and kappa ~0.6; supervised use and local validation first

Herkunft: research report by the remote-model agent (Clara on the hospital model hub) via the compact literature tool, checked by Claude Code 2026-10-18 (quellen-pruefen 0 wrong, 'Shows' spot checks against abstracts) · eigene Seite · Markdown

Aussage

  • No retrieved study shows a VLM performing autonomous QC triage on medical segmentation overlays. The closest published work is a benchmark of object-detection QC in which the medical domains were the hardest and where the authors explicitly recommend supervised, not autonomous, deployment [1].
  • On image-level QC tasks (chest radiograph technical QC, CT report consistency) multimodal LLMs are feasible but clearly task- and modality-dependent; the authors frame their own study as a feasibility benchmark supporting multi-centre validation "rather than immediate clinical deployment" [2].
  • On perceptual MRI attributes the picture is the same: a compact medical VLM (MedGemma-4B) reached 97% accuracy on modality and 80% on body part, but only 41% on orientation and 32% on contrast phase [4]. Single-slice MRI lesion detection by eight open VLMs showed high variability plus a strong positive-response (over-calling) tendency [3].
  • Agreement with experts is moderate, not high. ChatGPT-4o vs two radiologists on breast ultrasound BI-RADS gave κ = 0.59 / 0.62, while the two radiologists agreed with κ = 0.83; the model failed exactly in the intermediate categories and misread normal anatomy as lesions [5].
  • Image-only questions are much harder than text-only ones. GPT-4o/GPT-4V scored 73.5%/69.3% overall but only 55.6%/50.3% on image-based radiology exam questions, with poor self-calibration (AUC 0.64–0.66) [6].
  • In some studies passing descriptions to the model beats giving it the raw image: GPT-4-based (text) 43% vs GPT-4V (image) 8% in musculoskeletal radiology [7]; the authors conclude that appropriate descriptions of findings matter more than the pixels.
  • Hallucination is the dominant failure mode and is documented at rates of 5.4–17.4% for several medical VLMs in report generation, versus 0.1% for radiologists — the best model reached parity, others did not [12]. Reviews list hallucination as the main barrier to trust and propose multi-agent / RAG / uncertainty quantification as mitigations [13].
  • Overlays and image-displayed cues help, but they also create new error classes: adding key images raised workflow-error recall (63.2%→75.4%, 72.0%→83.6%) yet produced false positives on 18/60 error-free reports, mainly from body-marker misinterpretation — directly relevant to coloured overlay interpretation [11].
  • Laterality/orientation is a documented weak spot with concrete numbers: GPT-4V's 7/7 incorrect neuroradiology diagnoses were exactly the image-dominant ones, and orientation was the worst perceptual sub-task for MedGemma [4,8]. A generative-AI tool solved laterality error triage with 97.4–98.3% accuracy, but on a text-and-report task, not on raw segmentations [10].
  • Classical, non-VLM QC is a strong baseline and should be the comparison arm: uncertainty-based QC on 47,431 echocardiography studies [18], automatic error-region detection on knee/calf MRI with sensitivity 0.92–0.95 [15], and large-cohort organ-segmentation QC reporting ~90% error-free cases [16]. Traffic-light systems explicitly built for human-in-the-loop exist for segmentation quality [17].
  • Validate against the intended reference standard, with the reference standard being the human verdicts: LLM-as-a-judge work shows that judge reliability depends on rubric granularity and scale, and that criteria that work for human raters do not automatically transfer to an LLM judge [20,21]; CLAIM 2024 gives the reporting frame for such an evaluation [22].
  • A local, open-weight deployment is exactly the right choice for patient data: locally hosted open-weight LLMs at 7–33B parameters run on workstation-class hardware, keep images inside the institution, and are recommended together with vision-language / medical models such as MedGemma [23]; the same argument is made for privacy-preserving on-premises multimodal models [24], and MedGemma is published as an open medical VLM with a medical vision encoder [25].

Bottom line: a 27B local VLM is not yet evidence-backed as an autonomous QC gate on segmentation overlays. It is evidence-backed as a triage/human-in-the-loop component — provided it is evaluated against the human verdicts, its errors are reported per class (including laterality/orientation and hallucination), and a classical uncertainty/error-region baseline is run alongside it.

Practical recommendation (as written for our setup)

Local 27B VLM, montage of 9 axial slices with coloured segmentation outlines, 4-way verdict (pass / cortex wrong / subcortical wrong / unsure), n = 53 cases with human verdicts.

  1. Frame the model as pre-sorter, not decider. The evidence supports triage with human-in-the-loop only [1,19,23]. Design the workflow so that unsure and every model-flagged case goes to a human; measure how much human time the pre-sorting actually saves (the gatekeeper study measured this: ~30% gross reduction, 15.6% false removal [19]).
  2. Report per class and per error type. With 53 cases, a single global accuracy is uninformative. Report a 4×4 confusion matrix, per-class sensitivity/specificity, and the pattern of pass↔unsure confusion, since borderline classes are where VLM agreement collapses [5].
  3. Use κ against the human verdicts, and report the human–human κ as the reference ceiling [5]. Also report sensitivity for "any error" (a pre-sorter's most important number) separately from exact-class accuracy.
  4. Run a raw-vs-overlay ablation. Because no study isolates coloured outlines, treat overlay format as an experimental factor: (a) montage with coloured outlines, (b) raw montage without outlines, (c) montage + a short structured text description. Justification: overlays/markers both helped and produced new errors [11], and text descriptions outperformed raw images in one head-to-head [7].
  5. Probe the known failure modes explicitly. Include orientation/laterality checks (MedGemma: 41% [4]; laterality errors in GPT-4V [8]) and colour/overlay-marker probes (body-marker misinterpretation caused false positives [11]).
  6. Add the hallucination/consistency test cheaply. Ask the same montage twice with a paraphrase and a light perturbation and accept only agreeing verdicts; report the acceptance rate and the accuracy among accepted cases [14].
  7. Keep a non-VLM baseline in the same experiment — an uncertainty/error-region QC score [15,18] — so that the 27B model's added value is quantified rather than assumed.
  8. Report with CLAIM 2024 and pre-register the metrics, the reference standard and the exclusion rules [22].
  9. Deploy locally. 27B at Q4/low-bit quantization is in the range that runs on workstation-class hardware; document the quantization, the prompt, the model hash and the date, and be aware that quantization can cost accuracy [23].
  10. Do not use a closed cloud model on patient images, and do not let the model write anything into the clinical record — the retrieved evidence (hallucination rates, sycophancy, misread anatomy) is squarely against unsupervised clinical use [12,13,28,5].

Trägt nicht

Abstract-level evidence, a literature map rather than a systematic review. Open questions:

  • Is there any published evaluation of a VLM judging segmentation masks visually as overlay images (as opposed to QC of detection outputs or of image acquisition)? Not found in PubMed/Europe PMC for this report — this is the main gap.
  • Does a 9-slice montage help or hurt? Reading a single slice is documented [3]; multi-slice montage perception by VLMs is not.
  • How much does outline colour change the verdict? No retrieved evidence; it must be measured here.
  • What is a defensible acceptance threshold for a pre-sorter — sensitivity for "any error" ≥ ? at what false-removal cost? The gatekeeper study gives one data point (65.6% sensitivity, 15.6% false removal) but no consensus threshold [19].
  • Can quantization of a 27B model (needed for workstation deployment) be shown not to degrade orientation/laterality judgement? [23] flags quantization-induced accuracy loss as a governance risk; no medical-task numbers found.
  • Is a purpose-built VLM-IQA model (trained on quality reasoning) better than a general 27B chat VLM here? Non-medical evidence says yes for distortion tasks [29,30]; untested on segmentation overlays.
  • How should unsure be handled statistically — as a third class, or as abstention with its own acceptance-rate metric [14]?

Aussage

Every DEMDAS session is now scored automatically by a two-part quality gate (demdas-gm/qc_gate.py -> derivatives/demdas/gm_beispiel/qc_gate.csv, one row per BIDS session with a T1w). The gate combines

  1. the Euler number of the FreeSurfer cortical surface reconstruction, and
  2. the SynthSeg segmentation QC score.

A session is FLAGGED when either part fails; otherwise it is PASS. Flagged sessions are excluded from a second, parallel set of error statistics (columns *_qc on the volume-trend error page) so that the effect of the gate on SDC95/ICC and longitudinal noise is directly visible.

Why the Euler number. The Euler number of a surface counts its topological holes: holes = (2 - Euler)/2 per hemisphere. A clean, fully closed cortical surface has Euler = 2 and 0 holes; any defect, artefact or failed reconstruction step leaves a hole, so a more negative Euler number means a more defective surface. Rosen et al. (2018, NeuroImage 172:292-299, "Automated QC of FreeSurfer surface reconstruction") showed that the Euler number is the best automated correlate of a visual QC judgement of cortical surfaces, and that the cutoff is study-internal rather than a universal constant. We therefore do not hard-code a cutoff: it is derived from the cohort itself (below).

Cohort cutoff (derived, not fixed). Over the finished recon-all sessions the cutoff is the lower Tukey fence of euler_mean (the mean of the left and right hemisphere Euler numbers): Q1 - 1.5 * IQR. With the current cohort (55 finished recon-all sessions of 64) the cutoff is -107.75 (snapshot); a session is flagged on Euler when its euler_mean falls below it. In this cohort the fence sits at −107.75 (snapshot 2026-10-12) and one session (euler_mean −108) fell below it; that session also fails the SynthSeg arm. The Euler arm is in place and will fire as defective reconstructions appear.

SynthSeg QC score. mri_synthseg --qc predicts, for each tissue/structure, a quality score in [0, 1] (how well the segmentation matches what the network expects; higher = better). synthseg_qc_min is the minimum over all structure columns of seg/<id>_qc.csv (the weakest tissue for that session). The gate flags a session when synthseg_qc_min < 0.65. 0.65 is a working threshold chosen to match the existing manual QC practice on this dataset; it is to be confirmed against the SynthSeg README / the published --qc semantics before it is treated as a fixed standard.

Cohort result (2026-10-12, snapshot). 64 sessions; 55 with a finished reconstruction (Euler available), 9 still in recon-all (Euler empty, recon_all_finished = 0). 28 of 64 sessions are FLAGGED (27 by synthseg_qc_min < 0.65 alone, 1 by both synthseg_qc_min < 0.65 and euler_mean below the fence); 36 PASS. The flagged set is dominated by low-SynthSeg-QC sessions; these are exactly the sessions a reviewer would want to inspect first. The cutoff moves slightly as recon-all finishes more sessions (cohort-internal statistic), so the exact number of Euler flags can change between runs.

Where it is shown. - demdas-gm/qc_gate.py writes derivatives/demdas/gm_beispiel/qc_gate.csv (columns: subject, session, months, euler_lh, euler_rh, euler_mean, holes_mean, synthseg_qc_min, synthseg_qc_gm, synthseg_qc_wm, synthseg_qc_csf, recon_all_finished, flag_euler, flag_synthseg, flag_any). Idempotent; Euler results are cached so reruns are fast and recon-all progress is picked up on the next run. - Error statistics page (QUALITY_CONTROLL/DEMDAS/volumenverlauf/error_statistics.html), section 4 "QC gate": histogram of euler_mean with the cutoff line, scatter euler_mean vs synthseg_qc_min, and the table of flagged sessions; test-retest and longitudinal sections carry the QC-passed subset as extra *_qc columns. - GM axial QC page (QUALITY_CONTROLL/DEMDAS/gm_beispiel_qc/index.html): two new rows in the Session box, "Euler number (lh/rh)" and "QC gate" (PASS / FLAGGED: reason). - Excel: sheet QC_gate in DEMDAS_GM_Beispiel.xlsx (and error_statistics.xlsx).

Limits. The Euler number is only defined once recon-all has finished the surface steps; early sessions therefore carry an empty Euler and recon_all_finished = 0 and are not flagged on Euler (they are judged by SynthSeg alone when a segmentation exists). The gate is a triage aid, not a replacement for a visual check: a PASS means "nothing automated objected", a FLAG means "look at this one". The SynthSeg threshold 0.65 is provisional (see above). All numbers are cohort-level; no participant identifiers are published on the QC pages.

References. - Rosen B.R. et al. (2018). Automated QC of FreeSurfer surface reconstruction. NeuroImage 172:292-299. - SynthSeg: Maffei et al. (2018), and the mri_synthseg --qc documentation (threshold to be confirmed against the README).
