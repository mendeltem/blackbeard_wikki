Gaze tracking with the normal camera of a phone or laptop, in the browser: what accuracy is realistic, which features and calibration work, licence traps.

Kurzstand

Stand 2026-10-21. Prefill for chats and agents. Details in INDEX.md.

Moving after calibration (vorschlag): pose features fitted on a still calibration extrapolate wildly when the phone moves. Fix: iris offset along the image axes (measures gaze relative to the camera line, right under roll), a 5 s movement phase with the eyes on one dot, distance from iris size (11.7 mm, Vakunov & Lagun 2020), offset as a Kalman state updated by taps (Kalman 1960). Gyroscope not useful (measures phone vs room, not vs head). Toy simulation only; real phone not measured.

Camera-only gaze on phones (vorschlag): about 2-3 cm error is realistic with MediaPipe iris landmarks, head pose and a per-person ridge regression after a 9-dot calibration; trained CNNs with personal fine-tuning reach 0.5 cm (Valliappan 2020), WebGazer about 4 deg on laptops (Papoutsaki 2016). Enough for three large targets, not for words. Live app: https://mendeltem.github.io/camera-eye-tracker/. Real-phone error not measured yet.

Offen: measure the test-dot error on a real Android and iPhone.

BefundCamera-only gaze on a phone: about 2-3 cm with landmark features and a per-person regressionvorschlag2026-10-21

Herkunft: Claude (Alita), literature check 2026-10-21 and own app mendeltem/camera-eye-tracker (fce963a) · eigene Seite · Markdown

Aussage

Gaze from the normal front camera, in the browser, lands around 2-3 cm of error on a phone after a short calibration (own estimate from the studies below, not measured on a real phone yet). Trained CNNs with heavy personal calibration reach 0.5 cm, but only with large training sets and outlier users removed. The method that runs as a static page: MediaPipe Face Landmarker (iris points), iris offset measured in each eye's own frame, head pose and face position as extra features, ridge regression with lambda chosen by leave-one-dot-out cross-validation, One Euro smoothing, blink frames skipped, held-out test dots scored before they join the training set.

Study Setup Reported error
Krafka 2016, CVPR, arXiv:1606.05814 phone, iTracker CNN, with calibration 1.34 cm (1.71 cm without)
Valliappan 2020, Nat Commun 11:4553, doi:10.1038/s41467-020-18360-5 phone on a stand, CNN fine-tuned per person 0.46 cm; hand-held 0.50 cm
Papoutsaki 2016, IJCAI-16 3839-3845 laptop, WebGazer ridge on eye patches 4.17 deg in the lab
Semmelmann 2018, Behav Res Methods 50:451, doi:10.3758/s13428-017-0913-7 desktop, WebGazer online 207 px, 18 % of the screen
Davalos 2025, arXiv:2508.19544 (WebEyeTrack) browser, few-shot model 2.32 cm on GazeCapture, drifts over 20 min

Trägt

A browser demo or study that only needs which third of a phone screen someone looks at (three large targets, dwell selection), no installation, nothing uploaded. Camera position is free: features in the eye's own frame plus head pose let the calibration learn where the camera sits.

Trägt nicht

Reading single words, small targets, long sessions without recalibration (drift), glasses with reflections, dark rooms, a phone turned after calibration. WebGazer code is GPL-3.0 and GazeCapture data is research-only: do not copy either into an MIT repo.

Bruchindikator

The app's own test-dot error on a real phone well above 20 % of the screen diagonal in good light, or a study showing landmark-plus-ridge on phones far worse than 3 cm.

Ablesung

Live: https://mendeltem.github.io/camera-eye-tracker/ (2026-10-21). Headless Chromium with a fake camera passes the whole chain (10/10 steps, also against the live site); the test cannot measure accuracy because a still photo does not follow the dots. Real-phone error: not measured yet.

BefundMoving head or phone after calibration: fix the features and calibrate with movement, not the gyroscopevorschlag2026-10-21

Herkunft: Claude (Alita), own app mendeltem/camera-eye-tracker (b1c666a, 5f6e037), geometry and a toy simulation; user observation 2026-10-21 that the phone had to stay still · eigene Seite · Markdown

Aussage

A per-person regression fitted while head and phone stay still breaks when either moves, because its weights for head pose and face position were fitted on a tiny spread and a move multiplies them by values far outside it. Four parts address this without a trained network:

  1. Iris offset along the image axes, not in the eye's own frame. The iris sits in front of the eyeball centre, so its image offset measures the angle between the gaze and the eye-to-camera line. To first order this stays constant when the head moves or turns while the eyes hold a screen point, and it rotates with the screen when the head rolls, as the gaze point does. The eye-frame offset used before turns with the head and is wrong under roll.
  2. A movement phase in the calibration: about 5 s with the eyes on one dot while the head turns left, right, up and down. These frames teach the fit which feature changes (lids, blendshapes, pose) do not move the gaze point. Features are held within the calibrated range plus one standard deviation.
  3. Distance from the iris size. The visible iris is 11.7 ± 0.5 mm wide across a wide population (Vakunov & Lagun 2020, Google Research blog, 2020-08-06, https://research.google/blog/mediapipe-iris-real-time-iris-tracking-depth-estimation/; 4.3 % mean relative depth error against an iPhone depth sensor). The gaze offset from the camera scales with the distance, so predictions are scaled by iris size at calibration / iris size now.
  4. The slow offset as a Kalman state (Kalman 1960, J Basic Eng 82(1):35-45, doi:10.1115/1.3662552): a random walk whose variance grows with time and with the pose change since the last correction. A tap where the user looks is the measurement; after a large move the app shows a centre dot to look at and tap.

The phone gyroscope does not help here: it measures the phone turning in the room, while the gaze point depends on the phone relative to the head, which the camera already sees. Walking with the phone held still in front of the face reads as movement, and iOS needs an extra permission. A Kalman filter helps for drift, and for jitter only as much as the One Euro filter already does.

Trägt

Hand-held phone use with small head and grip changes after one calibration. In a toy simulation that follows the geometry above (tests/gaze.test.mjs), after a head turn the error stays at the still error with the movement phase (about 1 % of the screen) and grows to about 28 % of the screen without it.

Trägt nicht

Not measured on a real phone yet; the toy simulation only confirms the mechanism under its own assumptions. The eye-corner midpoint is not the eyeball centre, so large head turns leave a parallax error. One offset serves the whole screen and every pose. Turning the phone between portrait and landscape still needs a new calibration.

Bruchindikator

On a real phone, the test-dot error or tap errors after a deliberate head turn of about 10 degrees grow as much with the new calibration as with the old one (compare via the app's data export).

Ablesung

App 5f6e037 live at https://mendeltem.github.io/camera-eye-tracker/ (2026-10-21): unit tests 18/18, headless browser test 12/12 locally, from a fresh clone and against the live site. The result card offers "Save the data" (features, targets, times; no picture) so model variants can be compared on a real calibration.
